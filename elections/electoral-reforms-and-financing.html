Electoral Reforms and Financing

1. Meaning and Objectives of Electoral Reform

Electoral reforms are changes to the constitutional, statutory, administrative and political framework governing elections. Their purpose is to make elections free, fair, inclusive, transparent and genuinely competitive.

Electoral integrity requires more than orderly polling. It includes fair access to political competition, accurate electoral rolls, transparent political finance, informed voters, impartial election administration, responsible campaigning and effective remedies for violations.

Reform must balance several constitutional values: political equality, freedom of speech and association, representative government, federalism, privacy, transparency and the independence of election institutions.

2. Constitutional and Statutory Framework

  • Articles 324–329: Constitutional framework for election administration, electoral rolls, adult suffrage, legislative competence and election disputes.
  • Article 326: Adult suffrage for elections to the Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies.
  • Articles 102 and 191: Disqualifications for membership of Parliament and State Legislatures.
  • Tenth Schedule: Disqualification on grounds of defection.
  • Representation of the People Act, 1950: Allocation of seats and preparation of electoral rolls.
  • Representation of the People Act, 1951: Qualifications, disqualifications, conduct of elections, corrupt practices, election offences and election petitions.
  • Conduct of Elections Rules, 1961: Detailed procedures for nominations, polling, counting and related matters.
  • Election Symbols (Reservation and Allotment) Order, 1968: Recognition of political parties and allotment of election symbols.
  • Income-tax and company law: Relevant provisions governing political contributions, reporting and tax treatment.

3. Electoral Reform: Major Areas

Electoral rolls and voter access

  • Ensure accurate, inclusive and regularly updated electoral rolls.
  • Improve procedures for enrolment, correction, transfer and deletion of entries.
  • Prevent wrongful exclusion through accessible claims and objection mechanisms.
  • Address barriers faced by migrants, persons with disabilities, senior citizens and remote communities.
  • Improve voter awareness while protecting the secrecy and voluntariness of the vote.

Candidate selection and criminalisation

  • Strengthen disclosure of candidates' criminal antecedents, assets, liabilities and educational qualifications.
  • Improve public access to candidate information in a standardised and understandable form.
  • Encourage political parties to explain their reasons for selecting candidates with criminal cases, as required under the applicable directions and law.
  • Improve investigation and prosecution of serious offences without compromising the presumption of innocence or enabling politically motivated disqualification.

Campaign expenditure and political finance

  • Improve transparency of contributions to political parties.
  • Strengthen auditing and disclosure of party accounts.
  • Monitor indirect expenditure, third-party campaigning and digital advertising.
  • Address the gap between candidate expenditure limits and the wider expenditure of political parties and supporters.
  • Promote equitable political competition without restricting legitimate political expression.

Election administration

  • Strengthen the independence, capacity and credibility of the Election Commission of India.
  • Ensure impartial deployment of election personnel and law-enforcement agencies.
  • Improve transparency and public confidence in EVM and VVPAT procedures.
  • Provide timely and reasoned decisions on complaints and alleged violations.

4. Criminalisation of Politics

Criminalisation of politics refers to the increasing participation of persons facing serious criminal allegations in electoral competition and legislative institutions. It raises concerns about intimidation, misuse of money and coercive power, public trust and the rule of law.

Existing legal framework

  • Section 8 of the Representation of the People Act, 1951 provides for disqualification on conviction for specified offences.
  • Section 8(3) provides for disqualification where a person is convicted and sentenced to imprisonment of not less than two years, subject to the statutory provisions.
  • Candidate affidavits disclose criminal cases and other prescribed information under election law.
  • Political parties are subject to disclosure-related directions concerning candidates with criminal antecedents.

In Lily Thomas v. Union of India (2013), the Supreme Court invalidated the statutory provision that had allowed sitting legislators a period of protection from immediate disqualification following conviction. The judgment strengthened the operation of Section 8.

In Public Interest Foundation v. Union of India (2018), the Court issued directions requiring greater publicity of criminal antecedents of candidates and reasons for their selection by political parties.

Reform debate

Proposals to disqualify candidates merely upon the framing of charges raise difficult questions. A carefully designed legal threshold would need safeguards against false cases, political vendetta, delayed trials and the erosion of the presumption of innocence. Faster trials in serious cases, better disclosure and stronger party accountability are important complementary measures.

5. Political Finance: Meaning and Importance

Political finance refers to the funds received and spent by political parties, candidates and other participants in the electoral process. It supports campaigning, organisation, communication, research and political mobilisation.

Political finance is a legitimate part of democratic competition, but opaque or unequal funding can create undue influence, corruption risks and unequal access to political power.

Constitutional values involved

  • Article 19(1)(a): Political expression and the public's right to receive information.
  • Article 19(1)(c): Freedom of association, including political association.
  • Article 14: Equality and non-arbitrary regulation.
  • Free and fair elections: A basic feature of the constitutional framework, recognised in judicial decisions.

6. Legal Framework Governing Political Contributions

Representation of the People Act, 1951

  • Section 29B: Permits political parties to accept contributions from eligible persons and companies, subject to statutory restrictions.
  • Section 29C: Requires reporting of contributions above the prescribed threshold, in the manner laid down by law.
  • Section 29A: Provides for registration of political parties with the ECI.

Income-tax framework

Section 13A of the Income-tax Act, 1961 provides specified tax treatment for political parties subject to statutory conditions, including requirements relating to books, records, audit and reporting of contributions. Tax treatment is conditional and should not be understood as an unconditional exemption from disclosure or compliance.

Company law

Section 182 of the Companies Act, 2013 regulates political contributions by companies. Its provisions and amendments have been part of the wider debate on corporate political finance, disclosure and the influence of business interests.

Candidate expenditure

The RPA 1951 and Conduct of Elections Rules regulate election expenses of candidates, including the maintenance of accounts and filing of returns. Failure to lodge accounts without a good reason or justification can attract consequences under the statute. Candidate expenditure limits are prescribed by the ECI within the statutory framework.

7. Electoral Bonds: Legal Development

The Electoral Bond Scheme was introduced in 2018 as a mechanism through which eligible purchasers could buy bonds from the State Bank of India and donate them to eligible political parties. The scheme operated alongside changes to other laws governing political contributions and disclosure.

In Association for Democratic Reforms v. Union of India (2024), the Supreme Court unanimously declared the Electoral Bond Scheme unconstitutional and invalidated the relevant statutory amendments. The Court held that information about political contributions was relevant to voters' right to information under Article 19(1)(a). It also examined the potential for quid pro quo arrangements and the distinction between corporate and individual contributions.

The Court directed disclosure of information relating to electoral bonds by the State Bank of India and the Election Commission, subject to the directions in its judgment. The scheme is not a current valid route for political donations following the judgment.

Constitutional significance

  • Political funding transparency is connected to informed voting.
  • Opacity can prevent citizens from assessing possible relationships between donors, parties and public policy.
  • Corporate contributions raise questions about disproportionate influence and the relationship between economic and political power.
  • Transparency rules must be designed consistently with constitutional rights and legitimate privacy interests.

8. State Funding of Elections

State funding refers to public financial support for political parties or candidates to reduce dependence on private donations and limit the influence of money power.

Possible models

  • Direct funding: Public grants to eligible parties or candidates.
  • Indirect funding: Publicly provided facilities such as broadcasting time, venues or campaign infrastructure.
  • Matching grants: Public funds allocated in proportion to eligible private contributions.
  • Reimbursement: Repayment of specified and verified campaign expenses after an election.

Arguments in favour

  • Can reduce dependence on large private donors.
  • May improve fairness between candidates and parties with different financial resources.
  • Can encourage transparent accounting and formal political finance systems.

Concerns

  • Public funds may support parties without addressing opaque or illegal private funding.
  • Eligibility criteria may favour established parties and disadvantage new entrants.
  • State funding may impose a fiscal burden.
  • Public funding requires credible auditing, disclosure and enforcement institutions.

State funding is therefore not a substitute for transparency, expenditure monitoring and enforcement. It is one possible element of a wider political finance framework.

9. Simultaneous Elections

Simultaneous elections refer to holding elections to the Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies together or in a coordinated cycle. India's first general elections and State Assembly elections were held broadly together, but the cycle was disrupted by premature dissolution and political developments.

Arguments in favour

  • May reduce repeated deployment of administrative and security personnel.
  • Could reduce the frequency of election-related disruption to governance.
  • May reduce some categories of election expenditure.
  • Could create a more predictable electoral calendar.

Concerns

  • May affect federalism and the independent political mandate of State governments.
  • Premature dissolution of a House creates difficult questions about restoring a common cycle.
  • National issues may overshadow State-specific political choices.
  • Requires significant constitutional, statutory, administrative and logistical changes.
  • Could create uncertainty about the tenure of elected legislatures and the accountability of governments.

The issue should be assessed through constitutional design, federal balance, democratic accountability and practical feasibility rather than solely through administrative convenience.

10. Other Major Reform Proposals

  • Election Commission appointments: Transparent and credible appointment processes that strengthen public confidence in institutional independence.
  • Internal party democracy: Regular organisational elections, transparent candidate selection and accountable party finances.
  • Campaign regulation: Clear rules for online advertising, synthetic media, misinformation and third-party campaigning.
  • Election petitions: Faster disposal of election disputes without compromising due process.
  • Voter access: Better enrolment, accessible polling and measures to address disenfranchisement caused by migration or administrative errors.
  • Proportional representation debate: Examination of whether changes to electoral systems could improve representation while preserving accountability and workable government.
  • NOTA: Consideration of the limits of the None of the Above option and the distinction between registering disapproval and triggering a fresh election.
  • Technology: Transparent and verifiable use of EVMs, VVPAT and digital election systems.

11. Important Committees and Reports

  • Dinesh Goswami Committee on Electoral Reforms (1990): Examined election administration, expenditure, disqualification and electoral processes.
  • Indrajit Gupta Committee on State Funding of Elections (1998): Examined public funding and the conditions necessary for its introduction.
  • Law Commission, 170th Report (1999): Examined reform of the electoral framework, including political party functioning and election-related issues.
  • National Commission to Review the Working of the Constitution (2002): Considered reforms to constitutional and electoral institutions.
  • Second Administrative Reforms Commission: Addressed ethics in governance, public accountability and related institutional reforms.
  • Law Commission, 244th Report (2014): Examined electoral disqualification and the criminalisation of politics.
  • Law Commission, 255th Report (2015): Examined electoral reforms, political finance, party regulation and other structural issues.

12. Way Forward

  • Adopt a comprehensive political finance disclosure framework covering donations, party accounts, campaign expenditure and third-party spending.
  • Ensure timely, standardised and accessible disclosure of political contributions and audited accounts.
  • Strengthen independent auditing and enforcement, with proportionate penalties for non-compliance.
  • Improve transparency in candidate selection and political party functioning.
  • Strengthen the ECI's institutional capacity and credibility.
  • Address criminalisation through faster trials, effective disclosure and safeguards against misuse of disqualification provisions.
  • Develop proportionate rules for digital campaigning and political advertisements.
  • Ensure that any simultaneous election framework is consistent with federalism, parliamentary accountability and the constitutional tenure of legislatures.
  • Promote voter education and participation, particularly among underrepresented groups.

13. Important Distinctions

  • Candidate expenditure: Spending attributable to a candidate and regulated under the relevant statutory framework.
  • Party expenditure: Spending by political parties, subject to applicable reporting and regulatory requirements.
  • Political contribution: Money or other permitted support provided to a political party or candidate under the law.
  • State funding: Public support for eligible political activity or campaign expenses.
  • Disclosure: Publication of relevant information about funding and expenditure.
  • Audit: Independent examination of accounts and financial records.
  • Simultaneous elections: A proposal concerning the timing and coordination of elections, not a change to the electoral system itself.
