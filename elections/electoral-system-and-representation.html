1. Meaning and Importance of Electoral Representation

Electoral representation is the process through which citizens choose representatives to exercise public authority on their behalf. It connects popular sovereignty with representative institutions and makes government accountable to the electorate.

In a representative democracy, elections perform several functions:

  • Translate popular preferences into political representation.
  • Provide legitimacy to governments and legislatures.
  • Enable peaceful transfer of political power.
  • Hold elected representatives accountable.
  • Facilitate participation and political competition.
  • Provide a constitutional mechanism for inclusion and representation.

3. Adult Suffrage — Article 326

Article 326 provides that elections to the Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies are based on adult suffrage, subject to the Constitution and laws governing electoral registration and disqualification.

  • Every Indian citizen who is at least 18 years old on the qualifying date prescribed by law may be registered as an elector if otherwise qualified.
  • The voting age was reduced from 21 to 18 by the 61st Constitutional Amendment Act, 1988.
  • Registration and voting are subject to statutory conditions, including ordinary residence and disqualification provisions.
  • Adult suffrage expresses political equality through the principle of one eligible elector, one vote.

Article 326 concerns elections to the Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies. Local-body elections are governed by the separate constitutional framework in Parts IX and IX-A.

4. Universal Adult Franchise and Political Equality

Universal adult franchise was adopted at the commencement of the Constitution, despite widespread poverty, illiteracy and social inequalities. It represented a transformative commitment to political equality.

Its democratic significance includes:

  • Equal formal political status irrespective of wealth, education, caste, religion or gender.
  • Inclusion of historically marginalised communities in electoral politics.
  • Legitimacy through broad-based participation.
  • Opportunity to change governments peacefully.

Formal equality at the ballot box must be supported by effective access, voter awareness, freedom from intimidation and fair electoral competition.

5. First-Past-the-Post System

India generally uses the First-Past-the-Post (FPTP) system for direct elections to the Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies.

Under FPTP, the candidate who receives the highest number of valid votes in a constituency is elected. The winner does not need to secure more than half of all votes cast.

Key characteristics:

  • Single-member territorial constituencies for the relevant direct elections.
  • Each elector casts a vote for a candidate.
  • The candidate with the highest vote total wins.
  • Counting and declaration are relatively straightforward.

6. Advantages of FPTP

  • Simplicity: The method is easy for voters to understand and administer.
  • Constituency link: Each elected representative is associated with a defined territorial constituency.
  • Government formation: It can facilitate decisive legislative majorities.
  • Accountability: Voters can directly assess the performance of their constituency representative.
  • Administrative practicality: Counting and determining the winner are comparatively simple.

7. Limitations of FPTP

  • Disproportionality: The share of seats won by a party may differ substantially from its share of votes.
  • Wasted votes: Votes cast for losing candidates do not contribute to representation in that constituency.
  • Plurality victory: A candidate may win with substantially less than 50% of votes.
  • Geographical concentration: Parties with concentrated support may gain more seats than parties with dispersed support.
  • Underrepresentation: Smaller political groups may find it difficult to convert vote share into seats.
  • Strategic voting: Electors may vote for a viable candidate rather than their most preferred candidate.

These limitations generate debate about whether electoral outcomes adequately reflect the diversity of political preferences.

8. Proportional Representation

Proportional Representation (PR) seeks to allocate seats in a manner that more closely reflects the share of votes received by political parties or candidates.

India uses PR through the single transferable vote (STV) method for certain indirect elections, rather than as the general method for Lok Sabha and State Assembly elections.

Constitutional examples include:

  • Election of the President under Article 55.
  • Election of the Vice-President under Article 66.
  • Election of Rajya Sabha members under Article 80(4).
  • Election of members of State Legislative Councils under Article 171, where applicable.

9. Single Transferable Vote

Under the single transferable vote system, electors rank candidates in order of preference. A prescribed quota is used in elections where multiple seats are filled. Surplus votes and preferences are transferred according to the applicable counting rules.

The method is intended to achieve a form of proportional representation while allowing voters to express preferences among individual candidates.

Its use varies according to the constitutional or statutory election concerned. The President's election, Vice-President's election and Rajya Sabha elections have distinct electoral colleges and counting arrangements.

11. Constituencies and Territorial Representation

For direct territorial elections, the country or State is divided into constituencies from which representatives are elected.

Constituency design seeks to balance population, geography, administrative convenience and constitutional requirements.

  • Each constituency elects a specified number of representatives, ordinarily one in Lok Sabha and Assembly direct elections.
  • Constituency boundaries are determined through the constitutional and statutory delimitation framework.
  • Reserved constituencies ensure representation for specified communities without creating separate electorates.
  • Territorial representation links legislators to geographically defined electorates.

12. Delimitation and Equality of Representation

Delimitation is the process of determining or redrawing the boundaries of electoral constituencies and, where constitutionally permitted, allocating seats among territorial units.

Its objectives include:

  • Maintaining broadly comparable population representation.
  • Adjusting boundaries after demographic change.
  • Implementing constitutionally prescribed reservation of seats.
  • Reducing malapportionment and unequal vote weight.

Articles 82 and 170 govern readjustment of Lok Sabha and State Assembly constituencies after each relevant census, subject to constitutional amendments and the operative freeze provisions.

13. Reservation of Seats in Legislatures

Articles 330 and 332 provide for reservation of seats for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes in the Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies, respectively.

The system is based on territorial constituencies. All electors in a reserved constituency vote, but only candidates belonging to the constitutionally specified reserved category may contest that seat.

The 104th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2019 extended the period for SC and ST legislative seat reservation under Article 334 for the prescribed period, while discontinuing the provision for nomination of Anglo-Indian members to the Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies after the relevant constitutional period.

14. Reservation for Women in Legislatures

The Constitution (106th Amendment) Act, 2023 inserted Articles 330A and 332A and made related constitutional changes to provide for reservation of seats for women in the Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies. It also provides for reservation within the relevant SC/ST reserved seats.

The constitutional scheme links the commencement of the reservation provisions to the relevant census and subsequent delimitation exercise, as prescribed by the amendment. The reservation is designed to operate for the constitutionally specified period, subject to the provisions for extension.

Article 334A governs the duration and operation of the women's reservation framework. The amendment also provides for reservation in the Legislative Assembly of the National Capital Territory of Delhi through the relevant constitutional changes.

15. Representation in the Rajya Sabha

The Rajya Sabha represents the States and certain Union Territories at the Union level. Its elected members are chosen by the elected members of the respective State Legislative Assemblies through proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote.

Article 80 also provides for nominated members with special knowledge or practical experience in literature, science, art and social service.

Rajya Sabha representation is not equal among States; seat allocation is broadly related to population as prescribed in the Fourth Schedule.

16. Representation of Union Territories

Representation of Union Territories in Parliament is governed by the Constitution and parliamentary legislation. The manner of representation varies according to the constitutional status and legislative arrangements of each Union Territory.

Article 80 provides for representation of Union Territories in the Rajya Sabha as prescribed by Parliament. Article 81 and relevant laws govern representation in the Lok Sabha.

17. Electoral Rolls and the Right to Vote

Electoral rolls identify persons eligible to vote in a constituency. Article 325 requires one general electoral roll for every territorial constituency and prohibits exclusion on grounds only of religion, race, caste or sex.

The Representation of the People Act, 1950 and the Registration of Electors Rules, 1960 provide the principal statutory framework for electoral registration.

Important principles include:

  • Ordinary residence and registration in the appropriate constituency.
  • Age qualification and prescribed qualifying dates.
  • Protection against duplicate registration and wrongful exclusion.
  • Procedures for claims, objections and correction of electoral rolls.

18. Voting Rights and Electoral Participation

The right to vote is a statutory right regulated by election legislation, while the constitutional framework guarantees adult suffrage and protects electoral equality.

In People's Union for Civil Liberties v. Union of India (2003), the Supreme Court recognised the importance of the elector's freedom of expression in the context of knowing candidates' backgrounds.

In People's Union for Civil Liberties v. Union of India (2013), the Court directed introduction of the NOTA option in the voting process, recognising the elector's ability to register a choice of none of the above candidates.

NOTA does not operate as a right to reject all candidates and automatically trigger a fresh election under the general Lok Sabha and Assembly electoral framework.

19. Representation and Social Inclusion

Representative democracy requires more than the formal conduct of elections. It also raises questions about who can contest, who is elected and whose interests are reflected in public decision-making.

Important dimensions include:

  • Representation of women.
  • Representation of Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes.
  • Participation of minorities and marginalised groups.
  • Representation of geographically remote and underserved communities.
  • Accessibility for persons with disabilities.
  • Meaningful participation of first-time and young voters.

20. Major Challenges in Electoral Representation

  • Disproportionality between vote share and seat share.
  • Unequal influence of money and organisational resources.
  • Criminalisation and intimidation in electoral politics.
  • Underrepresentation of women and disadvantaged groups.
  • Unequal access to campaign platforms and political networks.
  • Voter exclusion due to registration errors or mobility.
  • Manipulation of electoral boundaries and concerns about delimitation fairness.
  • Low participation in some sections of the electorate.

21. Reform Priorities

  • Strengthen electoral-roll accuracy and accessible registration.
  • Improve transparency in political funding and campaign expenditure.
  • Promote fair opportunities for candidates and parties.
  • Strengthen enforcement against intimidation, bribery and hate speech.
  • Improve representation and participation of women and disadvantaged groups.
  • Ensure transparent, evidence-based delimitation.
  • Improve voter education and accessible polling facilities.
  • Evaluate electoral-system reform through constitutional, federal and democratic considerations.

22. Important Distinctions

  • FPTP vs proportional representation: FPTP elects the candidate with the highest votes in a constituency; PR seeks a closer relationship between vote share and seat allocation.
  • Direct vs indirect election: Direct elections involve voters choosing representatives; indirect elections use a designated electoral college.
  • Adult suffrage vs right to contest: The right to vote and eligibility to contest are governed by distinct constitutional and statutory requirements.
  • Reserved constituency vs separate electorate: In a reserved constituency all eligible electors vote, but candidate eligibility is restricted to the specified category; separate electorates involve separate voter constituencies.
  • Electoral roll vs constituency delimitation: The roll identifies electors; delimitation determines territorial constituency boundaries.

23. Integrated Understanding

India's electoral system combines universal adult suffrage, territorial constituencies, FPTP for direct Lok Sabha and Assembly elections, and proportional representation through STV for specified indirect elections.

The system must balance governability, accountability, political equality, social inclusion and federal representation. Electoral integrity therefore depends not only on the voting method but also on fair delimitation, accurate electoral rolls, campaign regulation and institutional independence.
