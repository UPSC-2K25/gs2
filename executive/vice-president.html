1. Constitutional Position

The Vice-President of India is the second-highest constitutional office in the Republic. The office is established under Article 63, which provides that there shall be a Vice-President of India.

The Vice-President performs two constitutionally distinct roles:

  • Serves as the ex officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha under Article 64.
  • Acts as President or discharges the President's functions when the office of President is vacant or the President is unable to discharge the functions of the office, under Article 65.

Unlike the President, the Vice-President is not ordinarily the constitutional head of the Union executive and does not exercise the general executive powers vested in the President under Article 53.

3. Election of the Vice-President

Article 66 provides for an indirect election by members of an electoral college consisting of members of both Houses of Parliament. The election is conducted through proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote, and voting is by secret ballot.

The electoral college includes:

  • Elected members of the Lok Sabha.
  • Nominated members of the Lok Sabha.
  • Elected members of the Rajya Sabha.
  • Nominated members of the Rajya Sabha.

State Legislative Assembly members and members of State Legislative Councils do not participate in the Vice-Presidential election.

This differs from the presidential electoral college, which includes elected members of State Legislative Assemblies and specified Union Territory Legislative Assemblies, but excludes nominated members.

4. Manner of Election and Counting of Votes

The election follows proportional representation through the single transferable vote. Each elector has one vote but may indicate preferences among candidates.

  • Electors mark candidates in order of preference.
  • All valid votes have equal value; there is no State-wise or member-wise weighted vote value.
  • First-preference votes are counted initially.
  • A candidate must obtain the prescribed quota to be elected.
  • If no candidate reaches the quota, the candidate with the lowest votes is eliminated and votes are transferred according to the next available preferences.
  • The process continues until a candidate reaches the required quota.

The equal value of votes reflects the fact that the Vice-President is elected by the parliamentary electoral college alone, without a separate component of State Legislative Assembly representation.

5. Qualifications for Election

Article 66(3) requires a candidate to:

  • Be a citizen of India.
  • Have completed 35 years of age.
  • Be qualified for election as a member of the Rajya Sabha.
  • Not hold an office of profit under the Union Government, a State Government or a local or other authority subject to the constitutional exceptions.

A person is not considered to hold an office of profit merely because the person is the President, Vice-President, Governor or a minister of the Union or a State.

The candidate must also satisfy the nomination and procedural requirements prescribed by the Presidential and Vice-Presidential Elections Act, 1952 and the rules made under it.

6. Conditions of Office

Article 66(2) provides that the Vice-President shall not be a member of either House of Parliament or a House of a State Legislature. If a member of either House is elected Vice-President, the seat is deemed vacant from the date the person enters office.

The Vice-President cannot hold any other office of profit while occupying the constitutional office. The role as ex officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha is part of the Vice-President's constitutional responsibilities, not a separate office of profit.

7. Term of Office

Article 67 provides for a five-year term.

  • The term begins from the date of entering office.
  • The Vice-President may resign by writing addressed to the President.
  • The Vice-President may be removed through the procedure under Article 67(b).
  • The Vice-President continues in office until the successor enters office, even after the five-year term expires.
  • The Constitution does not prescribe a maximum number of terms.

The Vice-President is eligible for re-election, subject to satisfaction of the constitutional qualifications and election procedure.

8. Removal of the Vice-President

Article 67(b) provides a special parliamentary procedure for removal. It is different from the impeachment procedure applicable to the President under Article 61.

  1. A resolution for removal must be moved in the Rajya Sabha.
  2. The resolution must be passed by a majority of all the then members of the Rajya Sabha.
  3. The Lok Sabha must agree to the resolution.
  4. At least 14 days' notice of the intention to move the resolution must be given.

The Constitution does not describe this procedure as impeachment. It is a removal mechanism requiring a special majority in the Rajya Sabha and agreement by the Lok Sabha.

The Vice-President, when presiding over the Rajya Sabha, does not preside during consideration of a resolution for the Vice-President's removal. Article 92 governs this restriction.

9. Oath or Affirmation

Under Article 69, before entering office, the Vice-President makes and subscribes an oath or affirmation before the President, or a person appointed by the President for that purpose.

The oath requires the Vice-President to bear true faith and allegiance to the Constitution of India and faithfully discharge the duties of the office.

The oath reflects the constitutional responsibility attached to both the presiding role in the Rajya Sabha and the potential responsibility of acting as President.

10. Vice-President as Ex Officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha

Article 64 provides that the Vice-President shall be ex officio Chairman of the Council of States. This is the Vice-President's principal regular function.

The Chairman's functions include:

  • Presiding over sittings of the Rajya Sabha.
  • Maintaining order and decorum in the House.
  • Interpreting and applying the Rules of Procedure and Conduct of Business.
  • Deciding points of order in accordance with parliamentary rules and conventions.
  • Regulating debates and ensuring orderly participation.
  • Putting questions to vote and announcing the results.
  • Exercising a casting vote in the event of an equality of votes.
  • Referring matters to committees where authorised by the rules.
  • Discharging other procedural and administrative functions assigned by the Constitution, laws and rules.

The Chairman is not ordinarily a member of the Rajya Sabha by virtue of the office. Consequently, the Chairman does not have an ordinary vote but may exercise a casting vote in the event of a tie under Article 100.

12. When the Vice-President Acts as President

Article 65 provides for the Vice-President to act as President when a vacancy occurs in the office of President by reason of death, resignation, removal or otherwise.

The Vice-President also discharges the functions of the President during the President's absence, illness or another inability.

When acting as President or discharging presidential functions, the Vice-President has the powers and immunities of the President and is entitled to the emoluments, allowances and privileges determined by Parliament for that purpose.

During this period, the Vice-President does not perform the duties of the Chairman of the Rajya Sabha. Those duties are performed by the Deputy Chairman or another person in accordance with the constitutional provisions and parliamentary rules.

13. Filling a Presidential Vacancy

When the office of President becomes vacant, the Vice-President acts as President until a new President is elected and enters office.

Article 62 requires the election to fill a casual vacancy in the office of President to be held as soon as possible and within six months from the date of occurrence of the vacancy. The newly elected President serves a full five-year term.

If the Vice-President's office is also vacant, or the Vice-President is unable to act, Article 70 enables Parliament to provide for the discharge of the President's functions in contingencies not otherwise covered by the constitutional chapter.

14. Powers and Limitations as Acting President

When the Vice-President acts as President under Article 65, the Vice-President discharges the functions of the President and enjoys the powers and immunities of that office for the relevant period.

  • The Vice-President acts within the constitutional powers of the President.
  • The parliamentary system and the aid-and-advice framework under Article 74 continue to apply.
  • The Vice-President does not acquire an independent political mandate.
  • The Vice-President cannot simultaneously perform the regular functions of the Rajya Sabha Chairman.

The arrangement ensures continuity of the constitutional executive headship without requiring a separate temporary presidential election.

16. Vice-President and Other Constitutional Presiding Officers

The Vice-President's role as Chairman of the Rajya Sabha should be distinguished from the Speaker of the Lok Sabha and the Deputy Chairman of the Rajya Sabha.

  • The Vice-President is elected by the parliamentary electoral college and becomes Chairman of the Rajya Sabha by virtue of the office.
  • The Speaker is elected by the Lok Sabha from among its members.
  • The Deputy Chairman is chosen by the Rajya Sabha from among its members.
  • The Chairman of the Rajya Sabha is not ordinarily a member of that House.
  • The Speaker and Deputy Chairman are members of their respective Houses.

These differences matter for voting, removal, presiding authority and the relationship between the presiding officer and the House.

17. Role in Parliamentary Democracy

  • Federal deliberation: The Rajya Sabha represents the States and provides a continuing forum for federal discussion.
  • Legislative continuity: As a permanent House, the Rajya Sabha is not subject to dissolution.
  • Procedural impartiality: The Chairman is expected to maintain order and fairness in House proceedings.
  • Constitutional continuity: The Vice-President can act as President when required.
  • Institutional balance: The office links parliamentary functioning with the constitutional succession mechanism for the Presidency.

18. Issues and Challenges

  • Presiding neutrality: The Chairman must maintain impartiality despite the Vice-President's election through a political electoral college.
  • House disruptions: Frequent disruptions can make the task of maintaining order and meaningful deliberation difficult.
  • Procedural controversy: Decisions on points of order, motions and admissibility may attract political disagreement.
  • Removal procedure: The Constitution provides a demanding parliamentary process but does not prescribe a detailed list of grounds for removal.
  • Dual responsibilities: The office combines a regular parliamentary role with the possibility of temporarily discharging the President's functions.
  • Public understanding: The Vice-President's role is sometimes incorrectly treated as equivalent to that of a Deputy President or an independent executive authority.

19. Reforms and the Way Forward

  • Maintain impartiality and consistency in presiding over the Rajya Sabha.
  • Strengthen parliamentary conventions and transparent procedural decisions.
  • Ensure adequate opportunities for debate and legislative scrutiny.
  • Promote respectful conduct and effective participation by members.
  • Clarify procedural expectations relating to the Chairman's role while preserving the authority of the House.
  • Protect the constitutional distinction between the Vice-President's parliamentary functions and the temporary discharge of presidential functions.

20. Important Distinctions for Revision

  • Election and removal: The Vice-President is elected under Article 66 and removed under Article 67(b); the President is elected under Articles 54–55 and removed through impeachment under Article 61.
  • Electoral college: Nominated MPs participate in the Vice-Presidential election but not in the presidential election.
  • State participation: State Legislative Assembly members participate in the presidential election but not in the Vice-Presidential election.
  • Chairman and Speaker: The Vice-President is ex officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha; the Lok Sabha elects its Speaker from among its members.
  • Acting President and President-elect: The Vice-President temporarily discharges presidential functions under Article 65; a person elected to fill a presidential vacancy receives a full five-year term.
  • Removal and impeachment: Article 67(b) does not describe the Vice-President's removal as impeachment.

21. Integrated Understanding

The Vice-President occupies a distinctive constitutional position that combines parliamentary leadership and continuity in the office of the President. As Chairman of the Rajya Sabha, the Vice-President contributes to orderly legislative deliberation. Under Article 65, the Vice-President provides a constitutional mechanism for continuity when the President cannot perform the functions of the office.

The office is therefore important to parliamentary functioning, bicameralism and constitutional succession. Its effectiveness depends on impartial presiding, respect for parliamentary conventions and faithful adherence to constitutional limits.
