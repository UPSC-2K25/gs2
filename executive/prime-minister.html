1. Constitutional Position

The Prime Minister is the head of the Union Council of Ministers and the central figure in India's parliamentary executive. Although the Constitution formally vests the executive power of the Union in the President under Article 53, the President ordinarily exercises constitutional functions on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister.

The Prime Minister provides political leadership, coordinates government policy, leads the Council of Ministers and serves as the principal link between the President and the Union Council of Ministers.

The office is not established through a single standalone constitutional article. Its position emerges from several provisions, particularly Articles 74, 75 and 78.

3. Appointment of the Prime Minister

Article 75(1) provides that the Prime Minister shall be appointed by the President. The Constitution does not expressly state that the Prime Minister must be the leader of the Lok Sabha majority, but parliamentary conventions require the President to appoint the person most likely to command the confidence of the Lok Sabha.

  • Where a political party or pre-election alliance has a clear majority, its recognised leader is ordinarily appointed.
  • Where no party has a clear majority, the President may assess which leader is most likely to secure majority support.
  • The appointee must demonstrate confidence of the Lok Sabha when the situation requires it.
  • In a situation of uncertainty, a floor test is the constitutionally appropriate method of determining majority support.

The President's discretion is limited by parliamentary conventions and the requirement that the government must enjoy the confidence of the elected House.

4. Appointment When the Prime Minister Is Not a Member of Parliament

The Constitution does not require a person to be a member of Parliament at the moment of appointment as Prime Minister. However, Article 75(5) provides that a minister who is not a member of either House of Parliament for six consecutive months ceases to be a minister at the expiration of that period.

This provision applies to the Prime Minister as a member of the Council of Ministers. Therefore, a non-member may be appointed but must become a member of either House within six months to continue in office.

The person must satisfy the relevant constitutional qualifications for membership of the House to which they are elected or nominated.

5. Oath of Office and Secrecy

Under Article 75(4), the President administers the oaths of office and secrecy to the Prime Minister and other ministers according to the forms set out in the Third Schedule.

The oath of office requires the Prime Minister to:

  • Bear true faith and allegiance to the Constitution of India.
  • Uphold the sovereignty and integrity of India.
  • Faithfully and conscientiously discharge the duties of the office.
  • Do right to all manner of people in accordance with the Constitution and law, without fear or favour, affection or ill-will.

The oath of secrecy requires the Prime Minister not to directly or indirectly communicate or reveal matters brought under consideration or known in the discharge of ministerial duties, except as required for the proper discharge of those duties.

6. Tenure and Resignation

The Constitution does not prescribe a fixed five-year term for the Prime Minister. The Prime Minister holds office during the pleasure of the President under Article 75(2), but this provision operates within the parliamentary framework of collective responsibility.

  • The Prime Minister normally continues as long as the government commands the confidence of the Lok Sabha.
  • The Prime Minister may resign by submitting a resignation to the President.
  • If the Prime Minister resigns, the Council of Ministers as a whole ordinarily ceases to hold office.
  • If the government loses a confidence vote, it must resign or follow the constitutionally appropriate course.
  • The President may appoint a successor who is capable of commanding the confidence of the Lok Sabha.

The expression "during the pleasure of the President" cannot be interpreted as giving the President an unrestricted personal power to dismiss a Prime Minister who continues to enjoy the confidence of the Lok Sabha.

7. Relationship with the President

The Prime Minister is the principal constitutional channel through which the Council of Ministers communicates with the President.

Article 78 places three specific duties on the Prime Minister:

  1. To communicate to the President all decisions of the Council of Ministers relating to the administration of the affairs of the Union and proposals for legislation.
  2. To furnish information relating to the administration of Union affairs and proposals for legislation as the President may call for.
  3. If the President so requires, to submit for consideration of the Council of Ministers any matter on which a minister has taken a decision but which has not been considered by the Council.

These duties preserve constitutional communication, collective consideration and informed presidential functioning.

8. Prime Minister and the Council of Ministers

Article 74 provides for a Council of Ministers with the Prime Minister at its head to aid and advise the President.

The Prime Minister's relationship with the Council includes:

  • Leading and coordinating the Council of Ministers.
  • Recommending the appointment of other ministers to the President.
  • Allocating and reshuffling ministerial portfolios.
  • Coordinating the work of different ministries.
  • Presiding over Cabinet meetings.
  • Resolving inter-ministerial differences through consultation and collective decision-making.
  • Communicating major policy decisions and priorities.
  • Advising the President on ministerial changes and resignation or removal of ministers.

The Council of Ministers is constitutionally responsible as a collective body, not merely as a collection of individually accountable ministers.

9. Collective Responsibility

Article 75(3) provides that the Council of Ministers shall be collectively responsible to the House of the People.

Its implications include:

  • The government must retain the confidence of the Lok Sabha.
  • Ministers are expected to publicly support collective Cabinet decisions.
  • A minister who cannot support a fundamental government decision is conventionally expected to resign.
  • Defeat on a matter of confidence requires the government to resign or otherwise resolve the loss of confidence in accordance with parliamentary conventions.
  • The Prime Minister's resignation ordinarily entails the resignation of the entire Council of Ministers.

Collective responsibility is the foundation of executive accountability in the parliamentary system.

10. Individual Responsibility of Ministers

Article 75(2) states that ministers hold office during the pleasure of the President. In parliamentary practice, the Prime Minister's advice is central to the appointment and continuation of individual ministers.

The Prime Minister can recommend a minister's resignation or removal and may reorganise portfolios. Individual responsibility allows the government to address ministerial misconduct, poor performance or loss of political confidence without necessarily bringing down the entire government.

11. Prime Minister as Leader of the Government

The Prime Minister provides overall political and administrative leadership to the Union Government. This leadership is exercised through constitutional authority, Cabinet processes, parliamentary accountability and political coordination.

  • Sets broad policy priorities and coordinates implementation.
  • Provides direction on major national and economic issues.
  • Coordinates ministries and departments.
  • Leads the government's legislative programme.
  • Represents the government in Parliament and in important national deliberations.
  • Provides political leadership during emergencies and major crises, subject to constitutional requirements.

12. Role in Cabinet Government

The Cabinet is the principal policy-making body within the Council of Ministers. The Prime Minister leads Cabinet deliberations and coordinates the collective exercise of executive authority.

Important functions include:

  • Determining major government policies.
  • Coordinating the activities of ministries.
  • Resolving important policy disagreements.
  • Considering legislative proposals and major administrative decisions.
  • Providing collective advice to the President.
  • Ensuring coordination between political priorities and administrative execution.

The Cabinet Secretariat supports inter-ministerial coordination and the transaction of government business under the applicable rules.

13. Legislative Role of the Prime Minister

  • Leads the government's legislative agenda.
  • Coordinates the introduction and passage of important government Bills.
  • Participates in parliamentary debates and responds to questions and criticism.
  • Explains major government policies to Parliament.
  • Advises on summoning and proroguing Parliament through the constitutional process.
  • Plays a central role in maintaining the government's majority and legislative support in the Lok Sabha.

The Prime Minister's legislative influence is subject to parliamentary procedures, constitutional limitations, bicameral scrutiny and judicial review where applicable.

14. Role in Parliamentary Accountability

The Prime Minister is accountable to Parliament through collective responsibility, debates, questions, motions, committee scrutiny and the requirement to retain the confidence of the Lok Sabha.

  • Must ensure the government retains majority support in the Lok Sabha.
  • Leads the government in responding to parliamentary criticism.
  • Is subject to scrutiny through questions and debates, in accordance with parliamentary rules.
  • Must ensure that the Council of Ministers remains collectively accountable.
  • Is expected to facilitate parliamentary scrutiny of major policy and legislative decisions.

Parliamentary accountability is not limited to formal confidence votes; it also depends on meaningful debate, transparency and access to information.

15. Administrative and Coordination Functions

The Prime Minister's administrative role is primarily one of leadership and coordination rather than direct management of every department.

  • Coordinates ministries with overlapping responsibilities.
  • Provides direction on cross-sectoral government priorities.
  • Supports coordination between Union ministries and State governments.
  • Oversees high-level administrative coordination through appropriate institutional mechanisms.
  • Promotes implementation monitoring and resolution of policy bottlenecks.

The Prime Minister's Office (PMO) provides secretarial and administrative assistance. It is not a constitutional ministry or a substitute for the statutory responsibilities of individual ministries and departments.

16. Role in Foreign Policy and National Security

The Prime Minister provides political leadership in foreign policy and national security, working through the Cabinet, relevant ministries and institutional mechanisms.

  • Represents India at major international and multilateral forums.
  • Provides strategic direction to foreign policy priorities.
  • Leads political consultations on important bilateral and regional issues.
  • Participates in high-level national security deliberations.
  • Coordinates the government's response to external security challenges.

These functions operate within the constitutional framework and the responsibilities of the President, Council of Ministers, Parliament and relevant ministries.

17. Role During Emergencies

The Prime Minister leads the Union Government's political and administrative response to emergencies, but emergency powers are constitutionally vested in the President and exercised within the prescribed procedures.

  • National Emergency: Article 352 requires the President to act on the written decision of the Union Cabinet.
  • President's Rule: Article 356 concerns a presidential proclamation based on the constitutionally prescribed grounds and process.
  • Financial Emergency: Article 360 provides for a presidential proclamation under the constitutional conditions.

The 44th Constitutional Amendment strengthened safeguards by requiring the written decision of the Union Cabinet for a National Emergency proclamation. The term "Cabinet" for this purpose refers to the Council consisting of the Prime Minister and other ministers of Cabinet rank appointed under Article 75.

The Prime Minister heads the Union Council of Ministers, whereas the Chief Minister heads the State Council of Ministers. The constitutional framework for the Chief Minister is primarily contained in Articles 163 and 164.

20. Constitutional Limit on the Size of the Council of Ministers

The 91st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003 introduced a limit on the size of the Council of Ministers.

  • Under Article 75(1A), the total number of ministers, including the Prime Minister, in the Union Council of Ministers shall not exceed 15 per cent of the total number of members of the Lok Sabha.
  • Under Article 75(1B), a member disqualified under the Tenth Schedule is also subject to restrictions on appointment as a minister for the period specified by the Constitution.

The amendment sought to discourage excessive expansion of ministries and strengthen political accountability.

21. Prime Minister and Coalition Government

In a coalition government, the Prime Minister's leadership depends not only on the largest party's parliamentary strength but also on the ability to maintain agreement among coalition partners.

  • Negotiates common policy priorities and coalition arrangements.
  • Coordinates ministerial representation and portfolio allocation.
  • Balances coalition commitments with collective Cabinet responsibility.
  • Maintains parliamentary support through consultation and negotiation.
  • Manages disagreements while preserving the government's ability to function.

Coalition arrangements do not alter the constitutional requirement that the Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha.

23. Issues and Challenges

  • Centralisation of power: Excessive concentration of decision-making can weaken Cabinet deliberation and ministerial autonomy.
  • Collective responsibility: The principle may become less meaningful if major decisions are taken without genuine collective discussion.
  • Parliamentary scrutiny: Disruptions, limited debate and inadequate legislative scrutiny can weaken accountability.
  • Coalition management: Coalition governments may face difficulties in reconciling diverse political priorities.
  • Institutional coordination: Overlapping responsibilities between ministries can delay policy implementation.
  • Executive transparency: Major policy decisions require adequate disclosure and public accountability.
  • Federal relations: Effective coordination with States is essential in a federal constitutional system.

24. Reforms and the Way Forward

  • Strengthen Cabinet deliberation and collective decision-making.
  • Preserve the autonomy and accountability of individual ministries.
  • Improve parliamentary scrutiny through debates, questions and committees.
  • Ensure transparent and evidence-based policy decisions.
  • Promote institutional coordination between Union and State governments.
  • Strengthen administrative accountability and implementation monitoring.
  • Maintain constitutional conventions governing the relationship between the President, Prime Minister and Council of Ministers.

25. Important Distinctions for Revision

  • Appointment and election: The President appoints the Prime Minister; the Prime Minister is not directly elected to that office by the public.
  • Tenure and term: The Prime Minister has no fixed five-year term as Prime Minister; continuation depends on parliamentary confidence and constitutional provisions.
  • Collective and individual responsibility: Article 75(3) concerns collective responsibility to the Lok Sabha; Article 75(2) concerns ministers holding office during the President's pleasure.
  • President and Prime Minister: The President is the formal constitutional head; the Prime Minister leads the political executive and Council of Ministers.
  • PMO and Cabinet Secretariat: The PMO provides assistance to the Prime Minister; the Cabinet Secretariat supports Cabinet coordination and government business.
  • Cabinet and Council of Ministers: The Cabinet is the smaller core decision-making body within the larger Council of Ministers.

26. Integrated Understanding

The Prime Minister is the central political institution of India's parliamentary executive. The office combines leadership of the Council of Ministers, coordination of government policy, legislative leadership and responsibility for maintaining parliamentary confidence.

The Prime Minister's authority is substantial but constitutionally conditioned. It operates through the Council of Ministers, collective responsibility, parliamentary scrutiny, federal coordination, constitutional conventions and judicially enforceable limits.

The effectiveness of the office therefore depends not merely on political leadership but also on Cabinet government, institutional restraint and accountability to Parliament.
