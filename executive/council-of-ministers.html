1. Meaning and Constitutional Position

The Union Council of Ministers is the constitutionally recognised body that aids and advises the President in the exercise of presidential functions. It is headed by the Prime Minister and forms the political executive at the Union level.

Article 74 provides for a Council of Ministers with the Prime Minister at its head to aid and advise the President. Article 75 deals with appointment, tenure, responsibility, qualifications and other conditions relating to ministers.

The Council is central to India's parliamentary system because executive authority is exercised through a government that remains collectively responsible to the directly elected Lok Sabha.

3. Constitutional Evolution

The Constitution originally provided for a Council of Ministers to aid and advise the President. The 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976 expressly made the President bound by that advice.

The 44th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1978 added an important safeguard: the President may require the Council of Ministers to reconsider its advice, generally or otherwise. After reconsideration, the President is bound to act in accordance with the advice tendered.

These amendments clarify the parliamentary character of the executive while retaining a limited constitutional reconsideration mechanism.

4. Composition of the Council of Ministers

The Council of Ministers consists of the Prime Minister and other ministers appointed by the President on the advice of the Prime Minister.

In conventional parliamentary practice, ministers are grouped into different ranks according to their responsibilities. The Constitution itself does not create these categories as separate constitutional offices.

  • Cabinet Ministers: Senior ministers who ordinarily head important ministries and participate in Cabinet decision-making.
  • Ministers of State with independent charge: Ministers who independently manage an assigned ministry or department without a Cabinet Minister above them for that portfolio.
  • Ministers of State: Ministers who assist Cabinet Ministers in their assigned ministries or departments.

The Prime Minister is the head of the Council and coordinates its work.

5. Council of Ministers and Cabinet

The Council of Ministers is the larger constitutional body. The Cabinet is its smaller and more influential decision-making core, consisting of ministers of Cabinet rank.

6. Appointment of Ministers

Article 75(1) provides that the Prime Minister is appointed by the President and other ministers are appointed by the President on the advice of the Prime Minister.

  • The Prime Minister recommends the names of persons to be appointed as ministers.
  • The President formally appoints them.
  • The President administers their oaths of office and secrecy.
  • Portfolios are allocated and reshuffled through the constitutional and governmental process.

The appointment process reflects the central position of the Prime Minister in the formation and functioning of the Union executive.

7. Qualifications and Membership of Parliament

The Constitution does not prescribe a separate, exhaustive list of qualifications for becoming a minister. A minister must satisfy the relevant constitutional requirements and disqualifications applicable to membership of Parliament.

Under Article 75(5), a person who is not a member of either House of Parliament for six consecutive months cannot continue as a minister after that period.

Thus, a non-member may be appointed as a minister, but must become a member of either House within six months to continue in office.

The minister's membership of either House does not alter collective responsibility to the Lok Sabha.

8. Oath of Office and Secrecy

Under Article 75(4), the President administers the oath of office and oath of secrecy to each minister in the forms specified in the Third Schedule.

The oath of office requires a minister to:

  • Bear true faith and allegiance to the Constitution of India.
  • Uphold the sovereignty and integrity of India.
  • Faithfully and conscientiously discharge the duties of the office.
  • Do right to all manner of people in accordance with the Constitution and law, without fear or favour, affection or ill-will.

The oath of secrecy protects confidential information obtained in the discharge of ministerial duties, subject to lawful requirements and the proper discharge of those duties.

9. Tenure and Pleasure of the President

Article 75(2) states that ministers hold office during the pleasure of the President. In the parliamentary system, this provision operates in accordance with the aid-and-advice framework and the leadership of the Prime Minister.

  • Individual ministers ordinarily continue while they retain the confidence of the Prime Minister.
  • The Prime Minister may recommend a minister's resignation or removal.
  • The President formally appoints and removes ministers in accordance with the constitutional process.
  • The Council as a whole must retain the confidence of the Lok Sabha.

The pleasure clause does not confer an independent political power on the President to dismiss ministers contrary to the parliamentary framework.

10. Collective Responsibility

Article 75(3) states that the Council of Ministers shall be collectively responsible to the House of the People. This is the defining principle of parliamentary executive accountability at the Union level.

Its key implications are:

  • The Council must collectively enjoy the confidence of the Lok Sabha.
  • Ministers stand or fall together on matters of government confidence.
  • Cabinet decisions are ordinarily defended collectively in public and in Parliament.
  • A minister who fundamentally disagrees with a collective decision is conventionally expected to resign rather than publicly undermine the government.
  • Loss of a confidence vote ordinarily requires the government to resign or otherwise resolve the loss of confidence through constitutional conventions.
  • The resignation of the Prime Minister ordinarily results in the resignation of the entire Council.

11. Individual Responsibility

Article 75(2), read with parliamentary conventions, supports the individual responsibility of ministers.

  • Each minister is responsible for the administration of the assigned ministry or department.
  • The Prime Minister may seek a minister's resignation or recommend removal.
  • Individual ministerial accountability allows corrective action without necessarily requiring the resignation of the whole Council.
  • Ministers are answerable to Parliament and must respond to questions and criticism concerning their departments.

Individual responsibility and collective responsibility are complementary principles: one addresses ministerial conduct and performance, while the other maintains the government's collective parliamentary accountability.

12. Council of Ministers and the President

Article 74 requires a Council of Ministers with the Prime Minister at its head to aid and advise the President.

The constitutional arrangement operates as follows:

  • The Council of Ministers formulates advice for the President.
  • The President ordinarily acts in accordance with that advice.
  • The President may require the Council to reconsider its advice once.
  • After reconsideration, the President must act in accordance with the advice tendered.

Article 74(2) further provides that the question whether any, and if so what, advice was tendered by ministers to the President shall not be inquired into in any court.

13. Functions of the Council of Ministers

Executive functions

  • Formulates and implements Union Government policies.
  • Coordinates administration across ministries and departments.
  • Advises the President on appointments and constitutional functions where required.
  • Supervises the implementation of laws and government programmes.

Legislative functions

  • Initiates government Bills and legislative proposals.
  • Coordinates the government's legislative programme.
  • Advises on summoning, proroguing and, where constitutionally appropriate, dissolving the Lok Sabha.
  • Frames delegated legislation under powers granted by Parliament.

Financial functions

  • Prepares and presents the Union Budget through the prescribed constitutional process.
  • Formulates taxation and expenditure policies.
  • Coordinates public financial management and fiscal priorities.

Policy and coordination functions

  • Determines major national policy priorities.
  • Coordinates economic, social and security policies.
  • Facilitates cooperation between Union and State governments.
  • Represents the government in domestic and international policy engagements.

14. Cabinet Committees

Cabinet Committees are important instruments for specialised consideration and coordination of government business. They are generally constituted under the Government of India (Transaction of Business) Rules, 1961.

  • They facilitate focused examination of specialised policy areas.
  • They reduce the burden on full Cabinet meetings.
  • They coordinate decisions involving multiple ministries.
  • They may be standing or ad hoc, depending on their purpose and terms of reference.
  • The Prime Minister plays a central role in their constitution, leadership and coordination, subject to the applicable rules.

Cabinet Committees are executive coordination mechanisms. They do not replace the constitutional responsibility of the Council of Ministers under Article 75(3).

15. Rules of Business

Article 77 provides the framework for the conduct of the business of the Government of India.

  • All executive action of the Government of India is expressed to be taken in the name of the President.
  • Orders and instruments made and executed in the President's name are authenticated in the prescribed manner.
  • The President makes rules for the more convenient transaction of government business and allocation of business among ministers.

The Government of India (Transaction of Business) Rules and Allocation of Business Rules operationalise this constitutional framework.

16. Limit on the Size of the Council

The 91st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003 introduced a constitutional ceiling on the size of the Union Council of Ministers.

  • Article 75(1A) provides that the total number of ministers, including the Prime Minister, shall not exceed 15 per cent of the total number of members of the Lok Sabha.
  • Article 75(1B) restricts the appointment of certain members disqualified under the Tenth Schedule as ministers for the constitutionally specified period.

These provisions seek to discourage excessive ministerial expansion and reduce incentives for political defections.

17. Parliamentary Rights of Ministers

Article 88 provides that every minister has the right to speak in, and otherwise take part in the proceedings of, either House of Parliament, any joint sitting and any parliamentary committee of which the minister may be named a member.

A minister who is not a member of a particular House may participate in its proceedings under Article 88 but cannot vote in that House solely by virtue of ministerial office.

18. Council of Ministers During a National Emergency

Article 352(3), inserted by the 44th Constitutional Amendment, requires that the President shall not issue a National Emergency proclamation unless the decision of the Union Cabinet to that effect has been communicated in writing.

For this purpose, the Constitution defines the Cabinet as the Council consisting of the Prime Minister and other ministers of Cabinet rank appointed under Article 75.

This requirement is an important safeguard against an emergency proclamation based solely on informal or individual executive advice.

19. Council of Ministers in Coalition Governments

In a coalition government, the Council of Ministers may include representatives of different political parties participating in the governing arrangement.

  • Portfolio allocation may reflect coalition negotiations.
  • Policy priorities may be shaped by a common programme or coalition agreement.
  • Inter-party consultation becomes important for maintaining stability.
  • Ministers remain subject to the same constitutional oath and parliamentary responsibility.
  • Coalition arrangements do not dilute the collective responsibility required by Article 75(3).

22. Issues and Challenges

  • Concentration of power: Excessive concentration of decision-making can weaken collective Cabinet government.
  • Cabinet deliberation: Limited consultation can reduce the quality of collective decisions.
  • Accountability gaps: Weak parliamentary scrutiny may reduce ministerial answerability.
  • Coalition pressures: Competing political priorities can affect policy coherence and stability.
  • Portfolio coordination: Overlapping ministerial jurisdictions can create administrative delays.
  • Ministerial size: Political pressures may encourage expansion within the constitutional ceiling.
  • Transparency: Confidentiality must be balanced with public accountability and access to information.

23. Reforms and the Way Forward

  • Strengthen Cabinet deliberation and meaningful collective decision-making.
  • Improve parliamentary oversight through questions, debates and committee scrutiny.
  • Clarify ministerial responsibilities and reduce overlapping jurisdiction.
  • Promote transparency in major policy decisions while protecting legitimate confidentiality.
  • Strengthen coordination between Union ministries and State governments.
  • Ensure that Cabinet Committees support, rather than bypass, collective constitutional responsibility.
  • Maintain the constitutional balance between executive effectiveness and parliamentary accountability.

24. Important Distinctions for Revision

  • Council of Ministers and Cabinet: The Council is the larger constitutional body; the Cabinet is its smaller core of senior ministers.
  • Collective and individual responsibility: Article 75(3) concerns collective responsibility to the Lok Sabha; Article 75(2) concerns ministerial tenure during the President's pleasure.
  • Appointment and portfolio allocation: The President formally appoints ministers on the Prime Minister's advice; portfolio allocation is handled through the government process.
  • Minister and Member of Parliament: A non-member may serve as a minister for up to six consecutive months under Article 75(5), subject to the constitutional requirement thereafter.
  • Cabinet and Cabinet Committees: The Cabinet is the central policy-making body; Cabinet Committees are specialised coordination mechanisms.
  • Article 74 and Article 75: Article 74 establishes the aid-and-advice relationship; Article 75 regulates the composition, appointment and responsibility of ministers.

25. Integrated Understanding

The Council of Ministers is the institutional foundation of India's parliamentary executive. It combines political leadership, collective decision-making and constitutional responsibility to the elected House of the People.

Its effectiveness depends on the relationship between the Prime Minister, Cabinet, individual ministries, Parliament and the President. The constitutional design seeks to ensure that executive authority remains effective while being subject to democratic accountability.
