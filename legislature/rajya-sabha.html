1. Constitutional Position and Significance

The Rajya Sabha, constitutionally known as the Council of States, is the upper House of the Parliament of India. It represents the States and Union Territories at the Union level and provides continuity, deliberation and a federal dimension to parliamentary law-making.

Article 79 establishes Parliament as consisting of the President and two Houses: the Council of States and the House of the People. The Rajya Sabha is not a directly elected chamber, nor is it a body representing State governments through their executives. Its elected members are chosen principally by elected members of State Legislative Assemblies.

Unlike the Lok Sabha, the Rajya Sabha is a permanent House. It is not subject to dissolution, and approximately one-third of its members retire every two years. This arrangement preserves institutional continuity while allowing periodic renewal.

2. Composition under Article 80

Article 80 prescribes the composition of the Council of States.

  • Not more than 238 representatives of the States and Union Territories.
  • 12 members nominated by the President.
  • The constitutional maximum strength is 250 members.

The present sanctioned strength is 245: 233 representatives of States and eligible Union Territories and 12 nominated members. The actual sitting strength may vary when seats are vacant.

Representation of States is not equal. The allocation of seats broadly reflects population, so larger States have more representatives than smaller States. This differs from the United States Senate model, where each State has equal representation.

Union Territory representation is determined by parliamentary law. At present, the National Capital Territory of Delhi, Puducherry and Jammu and Kashmir have representation in the Rajya Sabha under the applicable constitutional and statutory framework.

3. Nominated Members

Under Article 80(1)(a) read with Article 80(3), the President nominates 12 members who have special knowledge or practical experience in literature, science, art and social service.

The nomination mechanism is intended to bring expertise and public service experience into parliamentary deliberation. It provides a route for distinguished individuals to contribute even if they have not entered Parliament through an electoral contest.

Nominated members may participate in parliamentary proceedings and exercise voting rights, subject to constitutional exceptions. They do not participate in the election of the President, which is conducted by the constitutionally specified electoral college. They do participate in the election of the Vice-President, for which members of both Houses of Parliament are included.

A nominated member who joins a political party after the expiry of six months from the date on which they take their seat may incur disqualification under the Tenth Schedule. Joining a party within the first six months is treated differently under that Schedule.

4. Election of Representatives

Representatives of States in the Rajya Sabha are elected by the elected members of the State Legislative Assemblies through proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote, as provided by Article 80(4).

  • The election is indirect rather than by direct popular vote.
  • The system uses proportional representation and the single transferable vote.
  • Voting is conducted through a preferential ballot.
  • The applicable electoral procedure is governed by the Constitution and the Representation of the People Act, 1951, together with the relevant rules.

Representatives of Union Territories are chosen in the manner Parliament may prescribe by law under Article 80(5). The method depends on the relevant statutory provisions governing the particular Union Territory.

The system seeks to provide a degree of proportional representation to political parties in State Legislative Assemblies. It does not guarantee that every party will secure a seat, since outcomes depend on the number of vacancies, the strength of parties and the electoral quota.

5. Qualifications for Membership

Article 84 sets out the constitutional qualifications for membership of Parliament.

  • The person must be a citizen of India.
  • The person must make and subscribe the prescribed oath or affirmation.
  • The minimum age for membership of the Rajya Sabha is 30 years.
  • The person must possess other qualifications prescribed by Parliament by law.

The Representation of the People Act, 1951 provides statutory qualifications and other requirements. The minimum age of 30 distinguishes Rajya Sabha membership from Lok Sabha membership, for which the minimum age is 25 years.

6. Disqualifications and Vacation of Seats

Article 102 prescribes disqualifications for membership of either House of Parliament. These include holding an office of profit under the Union or a State, being of unsound mind as declared by a competent court, being an undischarged insolvent, loss of Indian citizenship or specified foreign allegiance, and statutory disqualifications.

The Representation of the People Act, 1951 provides additional grounds, including specified criminal convictions, corrupt practices and other electoral disqualifications.

The Tenth Schedule provides for disqualification on grounds of defection. The Speaker or Chairman decides questions under the Schedule, subject to judicial review.

Article 103 provides that questions of disqualification under Article 102(1) are decided by the President after obtaining the opinion of the Election Commission and acting according to that opinion.

Article 101 deals with vacation of seats, including dual membership, resignation and absence without permission for the constitutionally specified period. A member cannot simultaneously be a member of both Houses of Parliament.

7. Duration and Permanent Character

Article 83(1) provides that the Rajya Sabha is not subject to dissolution. As nearly as possible, one-third of its members retire every second year in accordance with parliamentary law.

The term of an individual elected member is ordinarily six years, subject to the statutory arrangements governing the allocation of terms and casual vacancies.

The permanent character of the House ensures that Parliament retains an institutional chamber even when the Lok Sabha is dissolved. It also allows members to develop continuity and experience in legislative scrutiny.

The Rajya Sabha's continuity does not mean that it can function independently of all constitutional requirements. Its meetings, quorum and legislative business remain subject to the Constitution and parliamentary rules.

8. Chairman and Deputy Chairman

The Vice-President of India is the ex officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha under Article 89. The House elects one of its members as Deputy Chairman.

The Chairman:

  • Presides over sittings of the Rajya Sabha.
  • Maintains order and ensures observance of parliamentary rules.
  • Decides procedural questions and regulates debate.
  • Exercises functions relating to the admissibility of motions and conduct of business.
  • Has a casting vote in the event of an equality of votes, but does not vote in the first instance.
  • Decides questions of disqualification under the Tenth Schedule in relation to Rajya Sabha members, subject to judicial review.

Article 90 deals with vacation, resignation and removal of the Deputy Chairman. Article 91 provides for the performance of the Chairman's duties during a vacancy or absence.

Article 92 provides that the Chairman or Deputy Chairman cannot preside while a resolution for their removal is under consideration. The Chairman, being the Vice-President, is removed from that office through the procedure prescribed by Article 67(b), which involves a resolution of the Rajya Sabha agreed to by the Lok Sabha and the prescribed notice.

Unlike the Speaker of the Lok Sabha, the Chairman of the Rajya Sabha is not a member of the House. The Chairman is the Vice-President, elected by members of both Houses of Parliament.

9. Legislative Powers

The Rajya Sabha participates in ordinary legislative business on broadly equal terms with the Lok Sabha.

  • An ordinary Bill may originate in either House.
  • Both Houses must ordinarily pass an ordinary Bill in identical form before it is presented for presidential assent.
  • Where a deadlock occurs over an ordinary Bill, Article 108 provides for a joint sitting in specified circumstances.
  • The Rajya Sabha can debate, amend, accept or reject an ordinary Bill.
  • Constitutional Amendment Bills must be passed separately by each House with the special majority prescribed by Article 368.

There is no joint sitting to resolve disagreement over a Constitutional Amendment Bill. The failure of either House to pass the required amendment prevents its enactment.

The Rajya Sabha's role in law-making is particularly important when it subjects legislation to a second examination and brings State-level, regional and specialist perspectives into national deliberation.

10. Financial Powers and Limitations

The Rajya Sabha has a more limited role than the Lok Sabha in financial matters, reflecting the principle that the directly elected House controls the government's financial responsibility.

  • A Money Bill cannot be introduced in the Rajya Sabha.
  • After the Lok Sabha passes a Money Bill, the Rajya Sabha may make recommendations within 14 days.
  • The Lok Sabha may accept or reject any or all recommendations.
  • If the Rajya Sabha does not return the Bill within 14 days, it is deemed to have been passed by both Houses in the form passed by the Lok Sabha.
  • The Rajya Sabha cannot vote on demands for grants under Article 113.

These limitations do not exclude the Rajya Sabha from all financial discussion. Members may debate the Budget, discuss economic policy and participate in the consideration of relevant financial legislation within the constitutional procedure.

It is important to distinguish a Money Bill under Article 110 from other Financial Bills under Article 117. The latter may involve a different legislative procedure and cannot automatically be treated as Money Bills.

11. Executive Accountability

The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha under Article 75(3), not to the Rajya Sabha. Therefore, the Rajya Sabha cannot remove the Union government by passing a no-confidence motion.

Nevertheless, the Rajya Sabha exercises meaningful oversight of the executive through:

  • Questions and supplementary questions.
  • Debates on government policy and administration.
  • Calling attention and other procedural devices.
  • Discussions on ministerial statements and public issues.
  • Parliamentary committees and scrutiny of expenditure.
  • Consideration of reports of constitutional and statutory authorities.

Ministers may be members of either House. A minister who is not a member of Parliament may remain a minister for no more than six consecutive months without becoming a member of either House, under Article 75(5).

Thus, the Rajya Sabha can question and scrutinise ministers, even though the government's collective survival depends on the confidence of the Lok Sabha.

12. Special Powers under Article 249

Article 249 gives the Rajya Sabha a distinctive role in the federal legislative structure.

If the Rajya Sabha declares by a resolution supported by not less than two-thirds of the members present and voting that it is necessary or expedient in the national interest for Parliament to legislate on a matter in the State List, Parliament becomes competent to legislate on that matter while the resolution remains in force.

  • The resolution is valid for a period not exceeding one year.
  • It may be renewed for further periods not exceeding one year each.
  • A law made under this authority ceases to have effect, to the extent of the relevant lack of competence, six months after the resolution ceases to operate, except as regards things done or omitted to be done before that time.

Article 249 is significant because it gives the Rajya Sabha a special constitutional role in enabling Parliament to legislate on State List matters in the national interest.

13. Special Powers under Article 312

Under Article 312, the Rajya Sabha may pass a resolution supported by not less than two-thirds of the members present and voting declaring that it is necessary or expedient in the national interest to create one or more All India Services common to the Union and the States.

Following such a resolution, Parliament may provide by law for the creation of the relevant All India Service and regulate recruitment and conditions of service.

This power reflects the Rajya Sabha's role in preserving the federal character of the Constitution while enabling nationally integrated administrative services.

The Indian Administrative Service and Indian Police Service are examples of All India Services. The Indian Forest Service was established subsequently under the constitutional and statutory framework.

14. Role in Presidential and Vice-Presidential Elections

The Rajya Sabha participates in important constitutional electoral processes.

  • Under Article 54, elected members of both Houses of Parliament participate in the election of the President, together with elected members of the Legislative Assemblies of States and the constitutionally specified Union Territories.
  • Under Article 66, members of both Houses of Parliament participate in the election of the Vice-President. Both elected and nominated members are included.

The Rajya Sabha also participates in the impeachment of the President under Article 61 and in other constitutional processes where the Constitution requires action by both Houses.

15. Role in Removal of Constitutional Functionaries

The Rajya Sabha participates in the removal or impeachment processes of certain constitutional authorities where the Constitution prescribes parliamentary action by both Houses.

  • President: impeachment under Article 61.
  • Judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts: removal under the relevant constitutional provisions and the Judges (Inquiry) Act, 1968.
  • Comptroller and Auditor General: removal in the same manner and on the same grounds as a Supreme Court judge, under Article 148(1).
  • Chief Election Commissioner: removal in the same manner and on the same grounds as a Supreme Court judge, under Article 324(5).

These procedures require the prescribed special majorities in each House. The Rajya Sabha therefore contributes to institutional independence and accountability.

16. Parliamentary Privileges

Article 105 provides for the powers, privileges and immunities of Parliament and its members. It protects freedom of speech in Parliament, subject to constitutional provisions and parliamentary rules.

Members enjoy immunity from court proceedings in respect of anything said or any vote given in Parliament or a committee thereof, within the scope of the constitutional protection.

Privileges exist to enable Parliament to function independently and effectively. They should not be interpreted as a general immunity from criminal law or from accountability for conduct unrelated to parliamentary functions.

In Sita Soren v. Union of India (2024), the Supreme Court held that parliamentary privilege does not protect legislators from prosecution for bribery connected with a vote or speech in the legislature.

17. Parliamentary Committees

The Rajya Sabha participates in the parliamentary committee system, which enables detailed scrutiny of legislation, public expenditure and administration.

Important committees include:

  • Public Accounts Committee.
  • Committee on Public Undertakings.
  • Department-related Standing Committees.
  • Committee on Government Assurances.
  • Committee on Petitions.
  • Committee of Privileges.
  • Rules Committee and other procedural committees.

Several committees are joint committees or have membership drawn from both Houses. The Rajya Sabha's participation ensures that committee scrutiny is not confined to the directly elected House.

Committee reports improve legislative understanding and executive accountability, although their recommendations are generally not binding.

19. Importance of the Rajya Sabha in Indian Federalism

The Rajya Sabha provides a federal dimension to the Union Parliament, but it is not a chamber of equal State representation.

  • It gives States an institutional voice in national legislation.
  • It provides a forum for regional concerns and State-level perspectives.
  • Its special powers under Articles 249 and 312 connect national interests with the federal distribution of powers.
  • Its permanent character offers continuity during periods of political transition in the Lok Sabha.
  • It may provide a forum for reconsideration of legislation and reduce the risk of hasty law-making.

However, party-based voting and the population-based allocation of seats mean that the Rajya Sabha does not operate as a chamber in which State governments necessarily vote as unified units. Its federal role is mediated through political parties and elected representatives.

20. Challenges and Reform Issues

  • Party polarisation and disruptions can reduce deliberative quality.
  • Frequent use of the party whip may limit independent scrutiny and debate.
  • Limited committee examination of Bills can weaken legislative oversight.
  • Population-based representation means that smaller States have fewer members, raising questions about the strength of their voice.
  • Indirect elections can create distance between members and ordinary voters.
  • Changes in party composition and political alignments may affect the House's ability to function as a revising chamber.
  • Use of Money Bill certification has raised concerns about the scope of the Rajya Sabha's legislative role.
  • Vacancies and delays in filling seats can affect representation.

Possible reforms include greater committee scrutiny, improved debate time, transparent legislative procedures, stronger research support, regular filling of vacancies and a constructive approach to federal deliberation.

22. Important Distinctions

  • Permanent House and permanent membership: The Rajya Sabha is permanent as an institution, but individual members have fixed terms and retire periodically.
  • Chairman and Speaker: The Chairman is the Vice-President and is not a member of the Rajya Sabha; the Lok Sabha Speaker is elected from among its members.
  • Money Bill and Financial Bill: A Money Bill has the restricted procedure under Articles 109–110; other Financial Bills are governed by Article 117 and may involve a different role for the Rajya Sabha.
  • Article 249 and Article 312: Article 249 enables parliamentary legislation on State List matters in the national interest; Article 312 enables Parliament to create All India Services after the prescribed Rajya Sabha resolution.
  • Election of President and Vice-President: Nominated members do not vote in the Presidential election but do participate in the Vice-Presidential election.

23. Integrated Understanding

The Rajya Sabha is designed to complement the Lok Sabha through continuity, legislative reconsideration, specialist contribution and federal representation. Its powers are broadly equal in ordinary legislation and constitutional amendments, but limited in financial matters and executive confidence.

Its special powers under Articles 249 and 312 demonstrate that it is not merely a revising chamber. It is a constitutionally significant institution in the distribution of legislative authority and the organisation of India's federal system.

The effectiveness of the Rajya Sabha depends on its ability to combine federal representation with informed deliberation, legislative scrutiny and responsible oversight.
