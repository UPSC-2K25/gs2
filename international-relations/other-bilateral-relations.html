1. Importance of Bilateral Relations in Indian Foreign Policy

India's bilateral relations extend beyond major-power diplomacy. Relations with neighbouring countries, West Asian partners, African States, Latin America, Central Asia and small island countries contribute to national security, energy access, trade, development partnerships and India's international influence.

India's approach is shaped by geography, historical connections, economic complementarities, diaspora links, development needs and strategic considerations.

2. India-West Asia Relations

West Asia is vital to India because of energy imports, trade, maritime routes, diaspora employment, investment and regional security. India's engagement has become more diversified, covering Gulf countries, Israel, Iran and other regional actors.

Gulf Cooperation Council countries

India's relations with countries such as the United Arab Emirates, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait, Oman and Bahrain involve energy, trade, investment, food security, defence and the welfare of Indian nationals.

  • Energy: Crude oil, natural gas and energy-transition partnerships.
  • Trade and investment: Logistics, infrastructure, financial services, technology and manufacturing.
  • Diaspora: Protection of workers' rights, consular assistance and remittance flows.
  • Security: Counter-terrorism, maritime security and defence dialogue.
  • Food security: Agricultural trade, food processing and supply-chain cooperation.

India-UAE relations

The UAE is a major trade, investment, logistics and diaspora partner. The Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement has provided a framework for expanding economic engagement. Cooperation also includes energy, digital technology, defence and connectivity.

India-Saudi Arabia relations

Saudi Arabia is important for energy security, investment, regional stability and the welfare of Indian workers. Cooperation increasingly includes renewable energy, infrastructure, logistics and strategic dialogue.

India-Oman relations

Oman's geographic position near important maritime routes gives it strategic significance. India and Oman cooperate in maritime security, defence, logistics, trade and energy.

India-Qatar relations

Qatar is an important supplier of natural gas and a partner in investment, trade and diaspora welfare. Energy security and long-term commercial arrangements are important dimensions of the relationship.

3. India-Israel Relations

India and Israel established full diplomatic relations in 1992. The relationship has expanded in defence, agriculture, water management, science, technology and innovation.

Areas of cooperation

  • Defence equipment, technology and security cooperation.
  • Agricultural research, micro-irrigation and water conservation.
  • Cyber security, artificial intelligence and innovation.
  • Water treatment, desalination and waste-water reuse.
  • Trade, investment and start-up collaboration.

Challenges

  • Balancing relations with Israel and the Palestinian people.
  • Instability and humanitarian crises in West Asia.
  • Regional security risks and wider geopolitical competition.
  • Ensuring that defence and technology cooperation supports India's long-term strategic needs.

India supports a negotiated two-State solution to the Israeli-Palestinian conflict, alongside its bilateral partnership with Israel. India's approach requires careful diplomacy, consistent humanitarian principles and attention to regional stability.

4. India-Iran Relations

Iran is strategically important because of its location near the Persian Gulf, the Strait of Hormuz and routes connecting West Asia with Central Asia and Afghanistan.

Major areas of cooperation

  • Connectivity through the Chabahar port project and related transport links.
  • Access to Afghanistan and Central Asian markets.
  • Historical civilisational and cultural connections.
  • Regional stability and counter-terrorism dialogue.
  • Potential cooperation in energy and trade, subject to sanctions and other constraints.

Challenges

  • International sanctions and financial transaction restrictions.
  • Regional conflict and security instability.
  • Balancing relations with Iran, Israel, the United States and Gulf partners.
  • Uncertainty in energy supplies and connectivity projects.

Way forward: Preserve strategic connectivity, comply with applicable international obligations, maintain diplomatic communication and avoid excessive dependence on any single route or partner.

5. India-Africa Relations

Africa is important for India's energy and mineral security, markets, development partnerships, maritime interests and support for reform of global governance. India and African countries also share historical links through anti-colonial struggles and South-South cooperation.

Major areas of engagement

  • Lines of credit, grants and development projects.
  • Education, scholarships, training and capacity building.
  • Healthcare, pharmaceuticals and vaccine cooperation.
  • Agriculture, food security and irrigation.
  • Digital public infrastructure and information technology.
  • Defence training, maritime security and counter-terrorism.
  • Trade, investment and access to critical minerals.

Institutional and diplomatic engagement

  • India-Africa Forum Summit process.
  • Engagement with the African Union and regional economic communities.
  • Cooperation through the United Nations, G20 and other multilateral platforms.

Challenges

  • Competition from other external powers.
  • Political instability and security challenges in parts of the continent.
  • Implementation delays and limited project monitoring capacity.
  • Need for greater private-sector investment and two-way trade.

Way forward: Adopt demand-driven development partnerships, improve project delivery, expand local capacity and promote African agency rather than treating the continent merely as a source of resources.

6. India-Central Asia Relations

Central Asia comprises Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan and Uzbekistan. The region is important for energy, minerals, connectivity, counter-terrorism and India's wider Eurasian engagement.

Areas of cooperation

  • Energy resources and critical minerals.
  • Trade, investment and industrial cooperation.
  • Connectivity through Iran and the International North-South Transport Corridor.
  • Counter-terrorism and regional security.
  • Education, healthcare, culture and capacity building.

Challenges

  • Absence of direct land connectivity through Pakistan.
  • Geographic distance and transport costs.
  • Competition among major external powers.
  • Security uncertainty in Afghanistan and the wider region.
  • Limited trade volumes relative to economic potential.

Way forward: Improve multimodal connectivity, deepen institutional engagement, promote digital and energy cooperation and build practical partnerships in education and security.

7. India-Latin America and the Caribbean

Latin America and the Caribbean offer opportunities for trade diversification, energy cooperation, agriculture, pharmaceuticals, critical minerals and South-South partnerships.

Major partners and areas

  • Brazil: Trade, agriculture, energy, technology and BRICS cooperation.
  • Argentina: Lithium, agriculture, energy and science cooperation.
  • Mexico: Manufacturing, pharmaceuticals, automotive components and trade.
  • Chile and Peru: Minerals, copper, lithium and investment opportunities.
  • Caribbean countries: Climate resilience, disaster management, health and development cooperation.

Challenges

  • Long geographic distances and high logistics costs.
  • Limited diplomatic and commercial presence in some markets.
  • Different regulatory systems and market conditions.
  • Need for better awareness of trade and investment opportunities.

Way forward: Strengthen business networks, trade agreements, air and maritime connectivity, diplomatic representation and cooperation in critical minerals and renewable energy.

8. India and Small Island Developing States

Small Island Developing States (SIDS) are particularly vulnerable to climate change, sea-level rise, extreme weather, ocean pollution and external economic shocks. Many are located in the Indian Ocean, Caribbean and Pacific.

India's engagement

  • Disaster response and humanitarian assistance.
  • Climate adaptation and resilient infrastructure.
  • Renewable energy and affordable technology.
  • Capacity building and digital cooperation.
  • Maritime security and protection of exclusive economic zones.
  • Support for stronger representation in international climate negotiations.

India's engagement with island partners is linked to its broader maritime vision and the principle of partnership based on mutual respect and locally identified development priorities.

9. India-Bangladesh Relations: Wider Bilateral Context

India-Bangladesh relations are also part of India's wider bilateral engagement beyond the immediate neighbourhood framework. Their importance includes trade, transit, energy, river management, border security and connectivity to India's north-eastern region.

  • Land Boundary Agreement and border management cooperation.
  • Trade and economic connectivity.
  • Cross-border electricity and energy cooperation.
  • River-water sharing and flood management.
  • Regional connectivity through road, rail, inland waterways and ports.

Long-term cooperation requires sensitivity to domestic political developments, equitable management of shared resources and sustained institutional dialogue.

10. Indian Diaspora as a Bilateral Link

The Indian diaspora contributes to bilateral relations through remittances, investment, entrepreneurship, cultural exchange, professional expertise and political engagement in host countries.

At the same time, diaspora welfare requires effective consular services, labour protections, legal assistance and emergency evacuation mechanisms. Diaspora diplomacy must be conducted in a manner that respects the sovereignty and domestic laws of host countries.

11. Cross-cutting Challenges in Bilateral Diplomacy

  • Geopolitical instability and conflicts in partner regions.
  • Sanctions, financial restrictions and competing external alignments.
  • Trade barriers and uneven economic complementarities.
  • Protection of Indian nationals and migrant workers.
  • Limited institutional and diplomatic resources.
  • Infrastructure and connectivity constraints.
  • Climate change and vulnerability of developing countries.
  • Need to balance strategic interests with humanitarian and developmental priorities.

12. Way Forward

  1. Adopt region-specific strategies rather than a uniform diplomatic template.
  2. Strengthen trade, investment and connectivity with emerging markets.
  3. Improve consular protection and labour welfare for Indian nationals abroad.
  4. Expand development partnerships based on local priorities and capacity building.
  5. Promote technology, health, education and renewable energy cooperation.
  6. Strengthen maritime and disaster-response partnerships with coastal and island States.
  7. Improve diplomatic presence, language expertise and regional research.
  8. Use bilateral relationships to build wider coalitions on climate, development and global governance.
