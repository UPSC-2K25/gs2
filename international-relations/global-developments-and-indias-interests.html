1. Understanding Global Developments

Global developments are political, economic, technological, environmental and security-related changes that influence relations among States and affect domestic policy. For India, international developments have direct consequences for national security, economic growth, energy access, food security, technology, migration and diplomatic influence.

India's foreign policy therefore combines immediate national interests with long-term objectives such as strategic autonomy, a stable neighbourhood, a multipolar international order and stronger representation for developing countries.

Global developments should be assessed through three connected questions:

  • What is changing in the international system?
  • How does the change affect India's interests and policy choices?
  • What diplomatic, economic, legal or institutional response is available to India?

2. Changing Global Order and Multipolarity

The post-Cold War international order has been shaped by the rise of new economic and military powers, the relative influence of established powers, regional conflicts and growing competition over technology and resources.

Major features

  • Increasing economic and strategic influence of Asian powers.
  • Greater role of regional groupings and issue-based coalitions.
  • Competition over global institutions, standards and supply chains.
  • Renewed importance of military alliances, deterrence and strategic partnerships.
  • Growing demand from developing countries for reform of global governance.

Implications for India: A multipolar environment creates opportunities for partnerships and diplomatic influence, but also requires India to manage competing expectations, protect strategic autonomy and avoid excessive dependence on any single power.

3. Major Power Competition

Competition among major powers increasingly extends beyond military strength to trade, technology, investment, critical minerals, infrastructure, standards and access to strategic regions.

  • United States and China: Strategic and technological competition affects trade, semiconductor supply chains, maritime security and the wider Indo-Pacific.
  • Russia and Western countries: Tensions affect European security, energy markets, sanctions, defence relationships and international diplomacy.
  • Europe's strategic role: European States and the European Union remain important in trade, climate policy, technology, development finance and global regulation.
  • Asian powers: Japan, South Korea and other regional actors influence technology, investment, maritime security and regional economic architecture.

India must assess each relationship independently, distinguishing areas of convergence from differences. Strategic autonomy does not mean isolation; it means preserving independent judgement while building useful partnerships.

4. Wars, Conflicts and International Security

Armed conflicts affect India's security environment even when India is not a direct participant. They can disrupt energy supplies, trade routes, food markets, expatriate communities and international diplomatic alignments.

Key dimensions

  • Territorial conflicts: Challenge sovereignty, international law and regional stability.
  • Great-power rivalry: Increases the risk of escalation and pressures States to choose diplomatic positions.
  • Maritime insecurity: Disrupts shipping through important routes and raises insurance and freight costs.
  • Terrorism and extremism: Create cross-border threats and humanitarian consequences.
  • Cyber operations: Target critical infrastructure, public institutions and economic systems.
  • Humanitarian crises: Require evacuation, relief, refugee protection and international coordination.

India's response may include diplomatic engagement, humanitarian assistance, evacuation operations, protection of citizens, support for peaceful settlement and coordination with international partners.

5. Global Economic Shifts and Supply-Chain Resilience

Globalisation has created interconnected production networks, but geopolitical tensions, pandemics, conflicts and trade restrictions have exposed vulnerabilities in concentrated supply chains.

Issues affecting India

  • Dependence on imported energy, fertilisers, electronics components and critical industrial inputs.
  • Exposure to shipping disruptions, higher freight costs and commodity-price volatility.
  • Trade barriers, export controls and sanctions affecting Indian businesses.
  • Competition for investment, manufacturing capacity and advanced technology.
  • Risks arising from concentration of production in a limited number of countries.

Policy responses

  • Diversify sources of critical imports and export markets.
  • Improve domestic manufacturing competitiveness and logistics.
  • Develop strategic reserves where economically and operationally justified.
  • Strengthen trade facilitation and infrastructure.
  • Build resilient partnerships with trusted suppliers without creating unnecessary economic fragmentation.

6. Energy Security and the Global Energy Transition

Energy security involves reliable, affordable and sustainable access to energy. India must balance growing energy demand, import dependence, climate commitments and the need for inclusive development.

External vulnerabilities

  • Concentration of oil and gas supplies in geopolitically sensitive regions.
  • Disruption of maritime chokepoints and shipping routes.
  • Volatility in global crude oil, gas and fertiliser prices.
  • Competition for critical minerals used in batteries, electric vehicles and renewable technologies.
  • Unequal access to finance and technology for the energy transition.

India's priorities

  • Diversify energy sources and suppliers.
  • Expand renewable energy, storage and grid infrastructure.
  • Promote energy efficiency and cleaner transport.
  • Develop domestic capabilities in solar, batteries and critical technologies.
  • Support affordable and equitable climate transitions.

India's climate diplomacy emphasises equity, climate justice, sustainable development and the principle of common but differentiated responsibilities and respective capabilities, in accordance with the relevant international framework.

7. Climate Change and Environmental Diplomacy

Climate change is a transboundary challenge affecting food systems, water security, public health, coastal communities, infrastructure and disaster risk. It also creates questions of finance, technology transfer, adaptation and historical responsibility.

Important international frameworks

  • UNFCCC: The foundational framework for international climate cooperation.
  • Kyoto Protocol: Established quantified emission-reduction commitments for specified developed-country parties.
  • Paris Agreement: Uses nationally determined contributions, transparency arrangements and periodic global stocktakes.
  • Convention on Biological Diversity: Addresses biodiversity conservation, sustainable use and benefit-sharing.

India's interests

  • Access to climate finance and affordable technology.
  • Protection of vulnerable populations and climate-sensitive livelihoods.
  • Recognition of differentiated national circumstances and development needs.
  • International cooperation on adaptation, disaster resilience and renewable energy.
  • Fair treatment of developing countries in climate-related trade measures.

8. Technology, Digital Governance and Cyber Security

Emerging technologies are changing economic competitiveness, military capabilities, public administration and international relations. Digital infrastructure and data have become strategic assets.

Key areas

  • Artificial intelligence: Raises questions of safety, accountability, employment, bias, national security and international standards.
  • Semiconductors: Are critical to telecommunications, defence, automobiles, computing and industrial production.
  • Cyber security: Requires protection of financial systems, energy networks, public services and critical infrastructure.
  • Data governance: Involves privacy, cross-border data flows, digital trade and regulatory sovereignty.
  • Telecommunications: 5G and future networks affect connectivity, security and technological dependence.
  • Space technology: Supports communications, navigation, weather forecasting, disaster management and strategic capabilities.

India's interests include affordable access, domestic innovation, trusted supply chains, international standard-setting and cooperation against cyber threats while protecting constitutional rights and legitimate national security concerns.

9. Global Health and Humanitarian Cooperation

Public health emergencies demonstrate that disease outbreaks can cross borders rapidly. International cooperation is essential for surveillance, research, vaccine development, access to medicines and emergency response.

  • Strengthening the World Health Organization and international health cooperation.
  • Improving disease surveillance and early-warning systems.
  • Building resilient health supply chains and pharmaceutical production.
  • Ensuring equitable access to vaccines, diagnostics and essential medicines.
  • Supporting humanitarian relief and disaster-response cooperation.

India's pharmaceutical industry, vaccine production capacity and experience in health cooperation provide opportunities for international engagement. These strengths also create responsibilities concerning quality, affordability and reliable supply.

10. Food Security, Agriculture and Critical Resources

Conflict, climate shocks, export restrictions, price volatility and supply-chain disruptions can threaten food security. India must protect domestic food availability while remaining engaged in international agricultural trade.

  • Secure and affordable supplies of fertilisers and agricultural inputs.
  • Resilient food supply chains and storage infrastructure.
  • Climate-resilient agriculture and efficient water use.
  • Balanced international trade rules that recognise food-security needs.
  • Cooperation on agricultural research, technology and early-warning systems.

Critical minerals are similarly important for clean energy, electronics, defence and advanced manufacturing. Diversified sourcing, recycling, domestic exploration and international partnerships can reduce vulnerabilities.

11. Global South and Reform of Global Governance

The term Global South broadly refers to developing countries and emerging economies that often share concerns about unequal global economic structures, representation and development opportunities. It is not a uniform political bloc.

India has sought to articulate concerns relating to development finance, debt sustainability, food and energy security, climate justice, technology access and reform of international institutions.

Institutional reform priorities

  • Greater representation of developing countries in international financial institutions.
  • Reform of the UN Security Council to reflect contemporary geopolitical realities.
  • Fairer international trade rules and development-oriented policy space.
  • Accessible climate finance and technology transfer.
  • More effective responses to sovereign debt distress.
  • Inclusive participation in digital and technological rule-making.

India's credibility depends on combining diplomatic advocacy with practical development cooperation and consistent engagement with diverse countries.

12. India's Strategic Interests: An Integrated Framework

  • Security: Protect territorial integrity, maritime interests, citizens abroad and critical infrastructure.
  • Economic prosperity: Secure trade access, investment, technology, energy and resilient supply chains.
  • Strategic autonomy: Preserve independent decision-making and avoid excessive dependence.
  • Neighbourhood stability: Support a peaceful, connected and economically stable South Asia.
  • Global influence: Increase India's role in international institutions and standard-setting.
  • Development: Secure finance, technology and partnerships that support inclusive domestic growth.
  • Human security: Protect food, health, water, climate and livelihood interests.
  • Values and reputation: Support international law, peaceful settlement, democratic participation and humanitarian cooperation.

13. Instruments Available to India's Foreign Policy

  • Bilateral diplomacy: Negotiations, strategic dialogues, agreements and high-level visits.
  • Multilateral diplomacy: Participation in the UN, G20, WTO, BRICS and other forums.
  • Development partnerships: Lines of credit, grants, capacity-building and infrastructure cooperation.
  • Economic instruments: Trade agreements, investment policy, market access and supply-chain partnerships.
  • Defence cooperation: Exercises, training, technology partnerships and maritime coordination.
  • Humanitarian assistance: Disaster relief, evacuation operations and emergency supplies.
  • Soft power: Education, culture, diaspora networks, public diplomacy and development experience.
  • International law: Treaty negotiations, peaceful dispute settlement and participation in rule-making.

14. Challenges in Protecting India's Interests

  • Balancing relations with competing major powers.
  • Limited financial, technological and military resources compared with established powers.
  • Regional instability and unresolved territorial disputes.
  • Dependence on imported energy, technology and critical components.
  • Fragmentation of global trade and multilateral institutions.
  • Climate vulnerability and unequal access to adaptation finance.
  • Rapid technological change and cyber threats.
  • Need for stronger coordination between domestic and foreign policy institutions.

15. Way Forward

  1. Develop a coherent long-term strategy linking diplomacy, economic policy, technology and national security.
  2. Strengthen domestic capabilities in manufacturing, research, energy and critical technologies.
  3. Diversify partnerships while maintaining strategic autonomy.
  4. Invest in diplomatic expertise, regional knowledge and international negotiation capacity.
  5. Build resilient supply chains and strengthen economic engagement with diverse regions.
  6. Support reform of global institutions through coalitions and constructive proposals.
  7. Deepen development partnerships based on local needs, sustainability and mutual benefit.
  8. Improve coordination between the Union, States, private sector, research institutions and diaspora networks where external interests overlap.
  9. Use international law and multilateral institutions to advance peaceful, rules-based cooperation.

16. Prelims Knowledge and Mains Analysis

Prelims focus

  • Major international institutions and groupings.
  • Climate and environmental frameworks.
  • Global trade, energy and maritime chokepoints.
  • Critical technologies and international regulatory initiatives.
  • Global South, multipolarity and strategic autonomy.

Mains focus

Analyse how geopolitical competition, economic fragmentation, climate change and technological transformation affect India's national interests. Assess India's ability to shape a multipolar order while preserving strategic autonomy and advancing the priorities of developing countries.

  • Security: Conflicts, terrorism, maritime routes and cyber threats.

  • Technology: AI, semiconductors, digital governance and space.
  • Human security: Health, food, migration and humanitarian assistance.
