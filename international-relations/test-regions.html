1. Role of Regional Organisations

Regional organisations facilitate cooperation among countries sharing geographic proximity, economic interests or common security concerns. They provide platforms for dialogue, trade facilitation, infrastructure development, conflict management and collective responses to transnational challenges.

For India, regional institutions are important instruments for neighbourhood diplomacy, connectivity, economic integration, maritime security and the promotion of a multipolar international order.

Major functions

  • Promote regional peace, stability and diplomatic dialogue.
  • Facilitate trade, investment and economic integration.
  • Develop transport, energy and digital connectivity.
  • Coordinate responses to terrorism, trafficking and other security threats.
  • Support disaster management, public health and climate resilience.
  • Strengthen the collective voice of member countries in global institutions.

2. South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation (SAARC)

SAARC was established in 1985 through the Dhaka Declaration. It seeks to promote the welfare of the peoples of South Asia and accelerate economic and social development through regional cooperation.

Members

Afghanistan, Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, Maldives, Nepal, Pakistan and Sri Lanka.

Objectives

  • Improve the quality of life and welfare of South Asian populations.
  • Promote economic growth, social progress and cultural development.
  • Strengthen collective self-reliance among member countries.
  • Encourage cooperation in science, technology, agriculture, health and education.
  • Promote mutual trust and understanding.

Institutional features

  • Decisions at summits and other major forums are taken on the basis of unanimity.
  • Bilateral and contentious issues are excluded from formal deliberations.
  • The Secretariat is located in Kathmandu, Nepal.
  • Sectoral cooperation is undertaken through ministerial meetings, technical committees and specialised mechanisms.

SAFTA

The South Asian Free Trade Area Agreement was signed in 2004 and entered into force in 2006. It seeks to reduce tariffs and promote trade among SAARC members, subject to agreed schedules and sensitive lists.

Achievements

  • Established a regional platform for sustained diplomatic engagement.
  • Created frameworks for preferential trade and sectoral cooperation.
  • Promoted collaboration in public health, education and disaster management.
  • Provided a regional institutional identity for South Asia.

Limitations

  • India-Pakistan tensions have repeatedly affected high-level engagement.
  • Unanimity makes decision-making difficult.
  • Intra-regional trade remains below its potential.
  • Weak transport connectivity and non-tariff barriers constrain integration.
  • Limited implementation and institutional capacity reduce effectiveness.

India's approach: India has continued to support practical regional cooperation while also pursuing subregional arrangements where progress is more feasible.

3. Bay of Bengal Initiative for Multi-Sectoral Technical and Economic Cooperation (BIMSTEC)

BIMSTEC was established in 1997 through the Bangkok Declaration. It connects countries around the Bay of Bengal and links South Asia with Southeast Asia.

Members

Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, Myanmar, Nepal, Sri Lanka and Thailand.

Significance

  • Connects India's eastern and north-eastern regions with Southeast Asia.
  • Provides an alternative platform for regional cooperation beyond the constraints affecting SAARC.
  • Promotes maritime, energy and transport connectivity around the Bay of Bengal.
  • Supports India's Act East Policy and neighbourhood engagement.

Priority areas

BIMSTEC's cooperation architecture covers areas including trade, investment and development; environment and climate change; security; agriculture and food security; people-to-people contact; science, technology and innovation; and connectivity.

Challenges

  • Political instability and security conditions in parts of the region.
  • Infrastructure gaps and slow implementation of connectivity projects.
  • Differences in economic capacity and national priorities.
  • Need to improve institutional resources and project coordination.

Way forward: Prioritise transport and energy connectivity, strengthen project implementation, promote trade facilitation and build practical cooperation on maritime security and disaster management.

4. Bangladesh-Bhutan-India-Nepal Initiative (BBIN)

BBIN is a subregional cooperation framework involving Bangladesh, Bhutan, India and Nepal. It focuses on practical cooperation in connectivity, transport, energy and trade facilitation.

Major areas

  • Cross-border road and passenger transport.
  • Movement of goods and regional logistics.
  • Cross-border electricity trade and energy cooperation.
  • Tourism and people-to-people connectivity.
  • Cooperation in water resources and environmental management.

The BBIN Motor Vehicles Agreement, signed in 2015, seeks to facilitate the movement of passenger, personal and cargo vehicles among participating countries, subject to implementation arrangements and domestic procedures.

Significance for India

  • Improves access between India's mainland and north-eastern region.
  • Strengthens regional supply chains and trade corridors.
  • Creates opportunities for hydropower trade and energy integration.
  • Demonstrates the value of flexible subregional cooperation.

Challenges

  • Differences in domestic regulatory systems.
  • Infrastructure and border-crossing bottlenecks.
  • Environmental and social concerns around large projects.
  • Need for coordinated customs, transit and vehicle standards.

5. Indian Ocean Rim Association (IORA)

IORA is a regional organisation established in 1997 to promote economic cooperation and sustainable development among countries bordering the Indian Ocean. Its Secretariat is located in Mauritius.

Areas of cooperation

  • Maritime safety and security.
  • Trade and investment facilitation.
  • Fisheries management and the blue economy.
  • Disaster risk management.
  • Academic, scientific and technological cooperation.
  • Tourism and cultural exchange.
  • Women's economic empowerment.

Importance for India

  • Supports India's maritime and SAGAR vision.
  • Strengthens engagement with African, Gulf and island partners.
  • Provides a platform for cooperation on maritime threats and ocean governance.
  • Promotes economic links across the Indian Ocean region.

Challenges: Large geographic coverage, diverse national capacities, strategic competition and the need to translate broad priorities into implementable projects.

6. Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN)

ASEAN was established in 1967 through the Bangkok Declaration. It is a central institution in Southeast Asian regional cooperation and an important pillar of India's Act East Policy.

Members

Brunei Darussalam, Cambodia, Indonesia, Laos, Malaysia, Myanmar, the Philippines, Singapore, Thailand and Vietnam. Timor-Leste's accession process and participation status should be distinguished from full membership when answering time-sensitive membership questions.

Principles and institutional features

  • Consensus-based decision-making.
  • Respect for sovereignty and territorial integrity.
  • Non-interference in internal affairs as a traditional organising principle.
  • Regional dialogue and peaceful settlement of disputes.
  • ASEAN centrality in the wider regional architecture.

ASEAN-led mechanisms

  • ASEAN Regional Forum: A platform for dialogue on political and security issues.
  • East Asia Summit: A leaders-led forum addressing strategic and economic questions.
  • ASEAN Defence Ministers' Meeting-Plus: Promotes practical defence and security cooperation with external partners.
  • ASEAN Plus Three: Connects ASEAN with China, Japan and South Korea for regional cooperation.

India-ASEAN relations

India's engagement covers trade, investment, maritime security, connectivity, education, digital cooperation, culture and strategic dialogue. The relationship is important for India's economic integration with Southeast Asia and its wider Indo-Pacific policy.

Challenges

  • Different national approaches to regional security issues.
  • South China Sea tensions and major-power competition.
  • Trade and connectivity barriers.
  • Political instability in parts of Southeast Asia.

7. Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO)

The SCO evolved from the Shanghai Five mechanism and was established in 2001. It provides a platform for cooperation on regional security, counter-terrorism, economic engagement and political dialogue across Eurasia.

India's participation

India became a full member in 2017. Its participation provides access to a major Eurasian forum involving Central Asian States, Russia, China and other members.

Major objectives and activities

  • Cooperation against terrorism, separatism and extremism as defined within the organisation's framework.
  • Regional security dialogue and confidence-building.
  • Economic, transport and connectivity cooperation.
  • Cultural, educational and people-to-people exchanges.

Importance for India

  • Engagement with Central Asia and Eurasia.
  • Regional counter-terrorism dialogue.
  • Opportunities for energy and connectivity cooperation.
  • Participation in a forum that includes major continental powers.

Challenges

  • Strategic differences with China.
  • Different approaches to connectivity and regional security.
  • Concerns about the political use of security terminology.
  • Need to balance engagement with India's wider partnerships.

8. Eurasian Economic Union (EAEU)

The Eurasian Economic Union is an economic integration organisation that came into existence in 2015. Its members are Armenia, Belarus, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan and Russia.

Objectives

  • Promote free movement of goods, services, capital and labour within the union framework.
  • Coordinate selected economic policies.
  • Develop a common customs and economic space.

Relevance to India

  • Potential for trade and investment diversification.
  • Access to Eurasian markets and resources.
  • Opportunities for industrial and transport cooperation.
  • Possible linkages with wider connectivity initiatives.

Any India-EAEU economic arrangement must address trade composition, regulatory compatibility, sanctions-related constraints and practical transport connectivity.

9. European Regional Integration: Relevance for India

The European Union is a distinctive model of regional integration, combining a customs union, single market and common institutions in several policy areas. It is not simply a conventional intergovernmental regional forum.

Lessons and relevance

  • Regional economic integration requires common rules and predictable institutions.
  • Connectivity and regulatory harmonisation can expand trade.
  • Dispute-resolution and enforcement mechanisms support institutional credibility.
  • Political commitment is necessary for deeper integration.

South Asian integration differs substantially in its political, economic and institutional context. The European experience offers comparative lessons rather than a model that can be copied without adaptation.

10. Comparative Overview of Regional Organisations

  • SAARC: South Asian regional cooperation; unanimity and bilateral-issue exclusion are important features.
  • BIMSTEC: Bay of Bengal cooperation linking South and Southeast Asia.
  • BBIN: Subregional connectivity and transport cooperation.
  • IORA: Indian Ocean economic, maritime and sustainable-development cooperation.
  • ASEAN: Southeast Asian regional integration and centrality in Indo-Pacific institutions.
  • SCO: Eurasian security, political and economic cooperation.
  • EAEU: Eurasian economic integration.
  • EU: Deep regional economic and political integration with supranational institutions in selected areas.

11. India's Strategy towards Regional Organisations

  • Neighbourhood First: Strengthen practical cooperation with immediate neighbours.
  • Act East: Deepen political, economic and strategic engagement with Southeast Asia.
  • SAGAR: Promote maritime security and sustainable development in the Indian Ocean.
  • Connectivity: Develop road, rail, port, energy and digital links.
  • Strategic autonomy: Participate in multiple institutions without becoming dependent on a single power bloc.
  • Development partnership: Support capacity building, public goods and locally prioritised projects.
  • Flexible cooperation: Use subregional and issue-based arrangements when larger institutions face political obstacles.

12. Challenges Facing Regionalism

  • Political disputes and lack of mutual trust.
  • Major-power rivalry and external strategic competition.
  • Weak institutional capacity and implementation deficits.
  • Unequal economic development among member countries.
  • Protectionism, non-tariff barriers and poor connectivity.
  • Transnational terrorism, trafficking and organised crime.
  • Climate change, natural disasters and environmental degradation.
  • Overlapping institutional memberships and competing priorities.

13. Way Forward

  1. Prioritise achievable, measurable projects over broad declarations.
  2. Improve institutional capacity, funding and monitoring.
  3. Promote trade facilitation and reduce non-tariff barriers.
  4. Strengthen cross-border transport, energy and digital infrastructure.
  5. Build trust through regular dialogue and practical cooperation.
  6. Improve regional disaster preparedness and public-health coordination.
  7. Support ASEAN centrality and an inclusive Indo-Pacific architecture.
  8. Use subregional mechanisms to complement wider regional institutions.
  9. Ensure that connectivity projects are transparent, sustainable and respectful of sovereignty.
