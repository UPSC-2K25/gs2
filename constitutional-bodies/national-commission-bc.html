National Commission for Backward Classes

1. Constitutional Position and Purpose

The National Commission for Backward Classes (NCBC) is a constitutional body established under Article 338B. It safeguards the interests of socially and educationally backward classes (SEBCs), examines complaints and safeguards, advises on socio-economic development and reports on the working of constitutional protections.

The Commission's present constitutional status emerged from the 102nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 2018. Before that amendment, the NCBC existed as a statutory body under the National Commission for Backward Classes Act, 1993.

  • Constitutional provision: Article 338B.
  • Constitutional status: Independent constitutional commission.
  • Related provisions: Articles 338B, 342A and 366(26C).
  • Core functions: Safeguards, complaints, development advice, monitoring and reporting.

2. Historical Evolution

  • First Backward Classes Commission: The Kaka Kalelkar Commission was appointed in 1953 under Article 340 and submitted its report in 1955.
  • Second Backward Classes Commission: The Mandal Commission was appointed in 1979 under Article 340 and submitted its report in 1980. Its recommendations became central to the national debate on reservation for Other Backward Classes.
  • Indra Sawhney judgment, 1992: The Supreme Court upheld reservation for OBCs in public employment subject to constitutional limits and directed the creation of a permanent body to examine inclusion and exclusion claims.
  • National Commission for Backward Classes Act, 1993: Established the NCBC as a statutory commission.
  • 102nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 2018: Inserted Article 338B, Article 342A and Article 366(26C), giving constitutional status to the NCBC and defining the constitutional framework for socially and educationally backward classes.
  • 105th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2021: Clarified and restored the power of States and Union Territories to maintain their own lists of socially and educationally backward classes for their own purposes, subject to the constitutional framework.

3. Composition and Appointment

Article 338B provides for a Commission consisting of a Chairperson, a Vice-Chairperson and three other Members.

  • The Chairperson, Vice-Chairperson and other Members are appointed by the President by warrant under his hand and seal.
  • Their conditions of service and tenure are determined by rules made by the President.
  • The Constitution does not itself prescribe a fixed tenure or a detailed selection committee mechanism.

The Commission's effectiveness depends on relevant expertise, institutional independence, adequate staffing and transparent processes for appointment and functioning.

4. Functions under Article 338B(5)

  1. Investigate and monitor all matters relating to safeguards provided for socially and educationally backward classes under the Constitution or under any other law or government order.
  2. Inquire into specific complaints concerning deprivation of rights and safeguards.
  3. Participate in and advise on the planning process for socio-economic development of socially and educationally backward classes and evaluate the progress of their development.
  4. Present reports to the President annually and at other times considered appropriate.
  5. Make recommendations concerning measures that should be taken by the Union or a State for effective implementation of safeguards and protection.
  6. Discharge other functions relating to the protection, welfare, development and advancement of socially and educationally backward classes as specified by the President, subject to law.

The NCBC is therefore concerned both with individual grievances and with systemic questions relating to educational opportunity, public employment, representation and the implementation of safeguards.

5. Investigative Powers under Article 338B(8)

While investigating a matter or inquiring into a complaint under Article 338B(5)(a) or (b), the Commission has the powers of a civil court trying a suit in respect of specified matters, including:

  • Summoning and enforcing the attendance of any person and examining them on oath.
  • Requiring the discovery and production of documents.
  • Receiving evidence on affidavits.
  • Requisitioning any public record or copy from any court or office.
  • Issuing commissions for the examination of witnesses and documents.
  • Any other matter prescribed by the President through rules.

These powers support fact-finding and inquiry. They do not transform the Commission into a regular court or make all its recommendations binding judicial orders.

6. Reports and Governmental Action

Under Article 338B(5)(d), the Commission submits reports to the President. Under Article 338B(6), the President causes the reports to be laid before each House of Parliament along with a memorandum explaining the action taken or proposed on recommendations relating to the Union and the reasons for non-acceptance, if any.

Under Article 338B(7), where a report concerns a matter relating to a State Government, a copy is forwarded to the Governor. The Governor causes it to be laid before the State Legislature along with a memorandum explaining action taken or proposed and reasons for non-acceptance, if any.

This arrangement enables legislative scrutiny of the Commission's findings and governmental responses. The practical effect of recommendations depends on the action taken by the relevant authorities.

7. Consultation under Article 338B(9)

Article 338B(9) requires the Union and every State Government to consult the Commission on all major policy matters affecting socially and educationally backward classes.

Consultation is relevant to policy decisions concerning:

  • Reservation in public employment and educational institutions.
  • Identification, inclusion and exclusion of communities in backward-class lists under the applicable constitutional framework.
  • Educational access, scholarships, hostels and skill development.
  • Economic empowerment, entrepreneurship and livelihood opportunities.
  • Monitoring of representation and the implementation of safeguards.

8. Constitutional Framework for Backward Classes

  • Article 15(4): Enables special provisions for the advancement of socially and educationally backward classes, SCs and STs.
  • Article 15(5): Enables special provisions for their admission to educational institutions, including private institutions, aided or unaided, other than minority educational institutions protected under Article 30(1).
  • Article 16(4): Enables reservation in appointments or posts in favour of backward classes of citizens that, in the opinion of the State, are not adequately represented in State services.
  • Article 16(4A): Enables reservation in promotion, with consequential seniority, for SCs and STs in specified circumstances.
  • Article 16(4B): Enables treatment of certain unfilled reserved vacancies as a separate class for the purpose of carrying forward vacancies.
  • Article 340: Enables the President to appoint a commission to investigate the conditions of socially and educationally backward classes and the difficulties under which they labour, and to make recommendations.
  • Article 338B: Establishes the NCBC and specifies its functions and powers.
  • Article 342A: Provides the constitutional framework for the Central List of socially and educationally backward classes and State or Union Territory lists for their own purposes.
  • Article 366(26C): Defines socially and educationally backward classes by reference to the meaning assigned in Article 342A.

9. Articles 342A and the Central and State Lists

The 102nd Constitutional Amendment inserted Article 342A. In its original form, the provision assigned the President the power to specify socially and educationally backward classes in relation to a State or Union Territory, after consultation with the Governor where applicable, and provided that Parliament could include or exclude classes from the Central List by law.

The Supreme Court's decision in Jaishri Laxmanrao Patil v. Chief Minister, Maharashtra (2021), concerning the Maratha reservation, interpreted the 102nd Amendment as affecting the power of States to identify SEBCs for their own purposes.

The 105th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2021 clarified the constitutional position and restored the power of States and Union Territories to prepare and maintain their own lists of socially and educationally backward classes for their own purposes. The amendment preserved the Central List framework for Union purposes.

Accordingly, it is important to distinguish:

  • Central List: Used for purposes of the Union Government and its institutions, within the constitutional framework.
  • State or Union Territory lists: Prepared and maintained by the respective State or Union Territory for its own purposes under the constitutional arrangement.
  • NCBC's role: A constitutional commission with safeguard, inquiry, advisory and reporting functions; it does not itself exercise Parliament's power to amend the Central List.

10. Mandal Commission and the Indra Sawhney Judgment

Mandal Commission

The Second Backward Classes Commission, chaired by B.P. Mandal, was appointed in 1979 under Article 340. It examined the social and educational backwardness of classes and recommended measures for their advancement. Its report, submitted in 1980, recommended reservation for Other Backward Classes in central government services.

Indra Sawhney v. Union of India (1992)

The Supreme Court's decision is a foundational judgment on reservation for backward classes. Key principles include:

  • Upheld the constitutional validity of reservation for OBCs in public employment under Article 16(4), subject to limitations.
  • Recognised the exclusion of the creamy layer from OBC reservation benefits in the context considered by the Court.
  • Generally treated 50 per cent as the ordinary ceiling on reservation, subject to exceptional circumstances recognised in the judgment.
  • Held that Article 16(4), as then interpreted, did not permit reservation in promotion.
  • Supported the creation of a permanent body to examine complaints of inclusion and non-inclusion in backward-class lists.

The judgment must be read with subsequent constitutional amendments and later decisions, including the amendments concerning reservation in promotion and the 102nd and 105th Amendments.

11. Creamy Layer and Substantive Equality

The creamy-layer principle distinguishes relatively advanced sections within a backward class from those who continue to experience the disadvantage that reservation is intended to address.

  • It is associated with the Supreme Court's decision in Indra Sawhney.
  • It seeks to ensure that benefits reach those who remain disadvantaged rather than being disproportionately captured by relatively advanced sections.
  • Its implementation requires clear criteria, reliable information and periodic review.
  • It raises questions about the relationship between social backwardness, economic position, educational attainment and representation.

The creamy-layer principle should not be confused with the identification of backward classes itself. Identification of a class and exclusion of advanced sections within that class are related but distinct questions.

12. Role in Identification and Inclusion Issues

Backward-class identification involves constitutional, empirical and administrative considerations. Relevant factors may include social status, educational disadvantage, traditional occupation, representation and regional conditions, as interpreted within the applicable legal framework.

The NCBC's constitutional role includes investigating and monitoring safeguards and examining complaints. Its recommendations and reports may inform governmental decisions, but the Commission does not independently amend the Central List or replace the constitutional and legislative processes governing lists.

Article 340 commissions, the NCBC, Union authorities and State authorities have different roles. These should not be treated as interchangeable institutions.

13. Relationship with Other Institutions

  • NCSC: Established under Article 338 to protect and monitor safeguards for Scheduled Castes.
  • NCST: Established under Article 338A to protect and monitor safeguards for Scheduled Tribes.
  • NCBC: Established under Article 338B to protect and monitor safeguards for socially and educationally backward classes.
  • Backward Classes Commissions under Article 340: Commissions appointed by the President to investigate the conditions of socially and educationally backward classes and make recommendations.
  • Union and State Governments: Exercise their respective policy, administrative and list-related responsibilities under the Constitution and applicable law.
  • Courts: Review the constitutionality and legality of reservation policies, classification and related administrative decisions.

14. Challenges and Limitations

  • Data gaps: Reliable and updated data on social and educational backwardness and representation can be limited or contested.
  • Identification disputes: Inclusion and exclusion decisions may generate political, legal and inter-community disputes.
  • Federal coordination: The relationship between Central and State lists requires clarity and effective coordination.
  • Creamy-layer implementation: Criteria and their application require periodic review and transparent administration.
  • Unequal access to benefits: Better-informed or relatively advanced groups may be better positioned to access opportunities.
  • Institutional capacity: Investigations, data analysis and monitoring require adequate staff, expertise and resources.
  • Recommendation follow-up: The Commission's influence depends substantially on governmental responses and implementation.
  • Changing social conditions: Backwardness is not static; regional and occupational changes require evidence-based reassessment.

15. Reforms and Way Forward

  1. Strengthen the NCBC's research, legal, statistical and field-investigation capacity.
  2. Promote transparent and evidence-based procedures for examining inclusion and exclusion questions.
  3. Improve the availability and quality of data on education, employment, representation and socio-economic disadvantage.
  4. Strengthen consultation between the Union, States and the Commission on major policy matters affecting backward classes.
  5. Ensure periodic review of creamy-layer criteria and their consistent application under the applicable legal framework.
  6. Improve monitoring of reservation implementation and the distribution of benefits among disadvantaged groups.
  7. Publish accessible annual reports with measurable indicators and action-taken follow-up.
  8. Promote transparent appointments, adequate resources and operational independence.
  9. Ensure that backward-class policy remains consistent with equality, social justice, federalism and constitutional limits.

16. Important Distinctions

  • Article 338B vs Article 340: Article 338B establishes the NCBC; Article 340 empowers the President to appoint commissions to investigate the conditions of backward classes.
  • Article 342A vs Article 341: Article 342A concerns socially and educationally backward classes; Article 341 concerns Scheduled Castes.
  • Article 342A vs Article 342: Article 342A concerns SEBC identification and lists; Article 342 concerns Scheduled Tribes.
  • NCBC vs NCSC/NCST: These are separate constitutional commissions under Articles 338B, 338 and 338A respectively.
  • Central List vs State list: The Central List serves Union purposes; States and Union Territories may maintain their own lists for their own purposes under the 105th Amendment framework.
  • Identification vs creamy-layer exclusion: Identification determines which classes qualify as backward; creamy-layer rules address exclusion of relatively advanced sections within the relevant class.
  • Commission recommendation vs final legal decision: The NCBC's investigative and advisory role is distinct from the constitutional and statutory powers exercised by governments and legislatures.

17. Integrated Understanding

The NCBC reflects the constitutional commitment to substantive equality through targeted safeguards for socially and educationally backward classes. Its role must be understood alongside the reservation framework, the Mandal Commission, judicial review, the creamy-layer principle and India's federal structure.

For GS-II analysis, connect Articles 15(4), 15(5), 16(4), 340, 338B and 342A with the 102nd and 105th Constitutional Amendments, Indra Sawhney and Jaishri Laxmanrao Patil. The central analytical issue is how evidence-based identification and effective safeguards can advance equality while respecting constitutional limits and federal responsibilities.
