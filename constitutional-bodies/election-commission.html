Election Commission of India

1. Constitutional Position and Significance

The Election Commission of India (ECI) is an independent constitutional authority responsible for the superintendence, direction and control of elections within the jurisdiction specified by Article 324 of the Constitution.

Free and fair elections are essential to representative democracy. The Election Commission is intended to ensure that electoral competition takes place under impartial administration, that eligible citizens can exercise their franchise and that electoral processes conform to the Constitution and applicable laws.

The ECI is not a department of the Union Government. It is a constitutional authority whose independence is necessary for the credibility of elections and peaceful democratic transfer of power.

2. Constitutional Framework

  • Article 324: Vests the superintendence, direction and control of elections to Parliament, State Legislatures, and the offices of President and Vice-President in the Election Commission.
  • Article 325: Provides for one general electoral roll for every territorial constituency and prohibits exclusion from electoral rolls on specified grounds of religion, race, caste or sex.
  • Article 326: Provides for elections to the Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies on the basis of adult suffrage, subject to constitutional qualifications and disqualifications.
  • Article 327: Empowers Parliament to make laws concerning elections to legislatures, subject to the Constitution.
  • Article 328: Empowers State Legislatures to make provisions concerning elections to their legislatures, subject to the Constitution and parliamentary law.
  • Article 329: Restricts judicial interference in electoral matters in the manner specified by the Constitution, including the requirement to challenge an election through an election petition.
  • Article 103: Provides for the President to decide specified questions of disqualification of Members of Parliament after obtaining the opinion of the Election Commission.
  • Article 192: Provides a corresponding arrangement for specified disqualification questions concerning State legislators, with the Governor obtaining the ECI's opinion.

3. Composition of the Election Commission

Article 324 provides that the Election Commission shall consist of the Chief Election Commissioner and such number of other Election Commissioners, if any, as the President may from time to time fix.

When other Election Commissioners are appointed, the Chief Election Commissioner acts as the Chairperson of the Commission. The Constitution permits a multi-member commission.

The ECI has functioned at different times as a single-member and multi-member body. Since 1993 it has operated as a multi-member commission, with the Chief Election Commissioner and other Election Commissioners participating in decision-making.

Article 324(4) also permits the President, after consultation with the Election Commission, to appoint Regional Commissioners to assist the Commission before general elections to the House of the People and State Legislative Assemblies and before the first general election and subsequent biennial elections to Legislative Councils, where applicable.

4. Appointment and Service Conditions

Article 324(2) provides for appointment of the Chief Election Commissioner and other Election Commissioners by the President, subject to any law made by Parliament on the matter.

The Chief Election Commissioner and other Election Commissioners (Appointment, Conditions of Service and Term of Office) Act, 2023 establishes the statutory appointment framework.

Selection process under the 2023 Act

  • A Search Committee, headed by the Minister of Law and Justice and including two other members not below the rank of Secretary to the Government of India, prepares a panel of five persons for consideration.
  • A Selection Committee recommends appointments to the President.
  • The Selection Committee consists of the Prime Minister as Chairperson, the Leader of Opposition in the Lok Sabha, and a Union Cabinet Minister nominated by the Prime Minister.
  • If the Leader of Opposition has not been formally recognised, the leader of the single largest opposition party in the Lok Sabha is treated as the Leader of Opposition for this purpose under the Act.
  • The Selection Committee may consider a person whose name is not included in the Search Committee's panel, subject to the Act.

The statutory framework followed the Supreme Court's interim arrangement in Anoop Baranwal v. Union of India (2023), which had provided for a selection committee consisting of the Prime Minister, Leader of Opposition in the Lok Sabha and Chief Justice of India until Parliament enacted a law.

5. Tenure and Removal Safeguards

Under the 2023 Act, the Chief Election Commissioner and Election Commissioners hold office for six years from the date they assume office or until attaining the age of 65 years, whichever is earlier. They are not eligible for reappointment.

The statutory framework also provides that an Election Commissioner or Regional Commissioner shall not be removed from office except on the recommendation of the Chief Election Commissioner.

Removal of the Chief Election Commissioner

Article 324(5) provides that the Chief Election Commissioner shall not be removed from office except in like manner and on like grounds as a Judge of the Supreme Court. The conditions of service of the Chief Election Commissioner cannot be varied to their disadvantage after appointment.

The removal procedure therefore requires the constitutionally prescribed parliamentary process for removal of a Supreme Court Judge, involving special majorities in both Houses on grounds of proved misbehaviour or incapacity.

Other Election Commissioners and Regional Commissioners do not enjoy the identical removal procedure of the Chief Election Commissioner. Their removal is subject to the constitutional requirement of the Chief Election Commissioner's recommendation.

6. Powers and Functions

Electoral administration

  • Superintendence, direction and control of elections within Article 324.
  • Preparation, revision and supervision of electoral rolls under the applicable legal framework.
  • Scheduling and administration of elections to Parliament and State Legislatures.
  • Conduct of elections to the offices of President and Vice-President.
  • Coordination and supervision of election machinery and observers.
  • Issuing directions and instructions to ensure orderly conduct of elections.

Political party and symbol-related functions

  • Registration of political parties under Section 29A of the Representation of the People Act, 1951.
  • Recognition of national and State parties under the Election Symbols (Reservation and Allotment) Order, 1968.
  • Reservation and allotment of election symbols under the applicable framework.
  • Resolution of specified disputes between rival groups or sections of recognised political parties under the Symbols Order.

Election expenditure and conduct

  • Monitoring candidate election expenditure under applicable law.
  • Issuing instructions and monitoring compliance with the Model Code of Conduct.
  • Monitoring campaign conduct and responding to complaints within its legal authority.
  • Promoting voter awareness and participation.

7. Scope and Limits of Article 324

Article 324 gives the ECI broad constitutional authority over the conduct of elections. However, this authority operates within the Constitution and laws enacted by Parliament and, where permitted, State Legislatures.

Where legislation occupies the field, the Commission must act consistently with that law. Its powers may fill gaps where legislation is silent, provided the action is consistent with the Constitution and statutory framework.

The Commission cannot use Article 324 to override an express statutory provision or create a power contrary to law. Its directions remain subject to constitutional judicial review.

8. Model Code of Conduct

The Model Code of Conduct (MCC) is a set of guidelines for political parties, candidates and governments in relation to election campaigns and conduct. It is issued and enforced by the ECI during the election period within its constitutional and statutory responsibilities.

Core areas

  • General standards of conduct and avoidance of appeals that aggravate communal or caste divisions.
  • Rules concerning meetings, processions and campaign activity.
  • Conduct on polling day and near polling stations.
  • Restrictions on the use of official machinery and public resources for electoral advantage.
  • Restrictions on announcements and policy decisions by governments during the election period, subject to the Code and applicable directions.

The MCC is not, as a whole, a separate statute enacted by Parliament. Some conduct addressed by it may independently constitute an offence or corrupt practice under election laws. Enforcement may involve warnings, notices, directions, referral to authorities or other action permitted by law.

9. Electoral Rolls and Voter Participation

Accurate electoral rolls are essential to the exercise of universal adult suffrage. The legal framework for electoral registration is primarily contained in the Representation of the People Act, 1950 and the Registration of Electors Rules, 1960.

  • Eligible citizens must be able to register in accordance with the law.
  • Electoral rolls must be prepared and revised through prescribed procedures.
  • Claims and objections must be considered by the designated electoral registration authorities.
  • Duplicate, inaccurate or wrongful entries should be addressed through lawful processes.
  • Voter awareness and accessible registration processes support inclusive participation.

The Commission supervises the electoral process, while specific statutory functions are performed by electoral registration officers and other designated authorities.

10. Election Machinery and Observers

Article 324(6) requires the President or Governor of a State, when requested by the Election Commission, to make available to the Commission such staff as may be necessary for discharge of its functions.

The ECI works with State and district election machinery, including Chief Electoral Officers, District Election Officers, Electoral Registration Officers, Returning Officers and other officials designated under election law.

Election observers may be deployed to monitor the conduct of elections, expenditure, law and order arrangements and other aspects of electoral administration. Their work supports oversight and reporting to the Commission.

11. Judicial Review and Important Judgments

Mohinder Singh Gill v. Chief Election Commissioner (1978)

The Supreme Court recognised the breadth of Article 324 and explained that the Election Commission has residual powers to act where the law does not make specific provision, subject to the Constitution and law.

A.C. Jose v. Sivan Pillai (1984)

The Court clarified that the Commission cannot override statutory rules and that its powers operate in areas not occupied by legislation.

T.N. Seshan v. Union of India (1995)

The Supreme Court upheld the constitutional validity of a multi-member Election Commission and explained the position of the Chief Election Commissioner and other Election Commissioners within the collective institution.

Union of India v. Association for Democratic Reforms (2002)

The Court recognised the voter's right to information about candidates as part of the freedom of expression under Article 19(1)(a), leading to disclosure requirements in the electoral process.

People's Union for Civil Liberties v. Union of India (2003)

The Court reaffirmed the importance of voter information and considered the constitutional implications of disclosure requirements and electoral transparency.

People's Union for Civil Liberties v. Union of India (2013)

The Supreme Court directed the introduction of the NOTA option in voting machines and ballot papers, recognising the importance of voter choice and secrecy.

Anoop Baranwal v. Union of India (2023)

The Court prescribed an interim appointment committee for Election Commissioners until Parliament enacted a law under Article 324(2).

12. State Election Commissions: Distinction

The Election Commission of India must be distinguished from State Election Commissions.

  • Article 324: Governs the ECI's jurisdiction over elections to Parliament, State Legislatures and the offices of President and Vice-President.
  • Article 243K: Provides for State Election Commissions to supervise, direct and control elections to Panchayats.
  • Article 243ZA: Applies the State Election Commission framework to municipal elections.
  • Appointment: The State Election Commissioner is appointed by the Governor, subject to constitutional safeguards.
  • Removal: The State Election Commissioner cannot be removed except in like manner and on like grounds as a Judge of a High Court; service conditions cannot be varied to their disadvantage after appointment.

The ECI and State Election Commissions are separate constitutional authorities with distinct electoral jurisdictions.

13. Challenges to the Election Commission

  • Appointment debate: Concerns about executive influence and the need for a transparent, broadly trusted selection process.
  • Institutional capacity: Increasing complexity of campaigns, digital media, expenditure and electoral logistics.
  • Enforcement consistency: Need for timely, transparent and even-handed action on complaints.
  • Money power: Difficulty in detecting concealed expenditure, third-party campaigning and illicit inducements.
  • Criminalisation of politics: Challenges arising from candidates facing serious criminal cases and the limits of existing disqualification law.
  • Disinformation: Online misinformation, deepfakes, synthetic media and coordinated manipulation.
  • Electoral roll integrity: Balancing accuracy with protection against wrongful exclusion.
  • Internal party democracy: Limited transparency in candidate selection and party functioning.
  • Trust and neutrality: Public confidence depends on impartial conduct and transparent reasoning.

14. Reforms and the Way Forward

  • Ensure transparent and credible appointments while protecting constitutional independence.
  • Provide adequate staffing, technology, training and financial resources.
  • Strengthen disclosure and scrutiny of political finance and election expenditure.
  • Improve transparency in MCC enforcement, including publication of reasoned decisions.
  • Develop clear and timely procedures for complaints concerning digital campaigning and misinformation.
  • Protect electoral rolls from wrongful inclusion and exclusion through transparent verification and appeal mechanisms.
  • Strengthen voter education and accessibility for persons with disabilities, senior citizens and remote communities.
  • Promote greater transparency in political party registration, recognition and internal functioning.
  • Improve coordination with law enforcement and other statutory authorities while preserving the Commission's independence.
  • Ensure that election technology is secure, auditable and supported by transparent procedures.

15. Important Distinctions

  • ECI and State Election Commission: The former administers national and State-level elections specified in Article 324; the latter administers Panchayat and municipal elections.
  • Registration and recognition: Registration under Section 29A of the RPA 1951 differs from recognition as a national or State party under the Symbols Order.
  • Constitutional power and statutory power: Article 324 provides broad constitutional authority, while election statutes prescribe detailed procedures and powers.
  • MCC and election law: The MCC is a set of election-period guidelines; statutory offences and corrupt practices are separately governed by law.
  • CEC and other Election Commissioners: The Constitution provides distinct removal safeguards for the CEC and other Election Commissioners.

16. Integrated Understanding

The Election Commission is a central institution in India's constitutional democracy. Its credibility depends not only on formal powers under Article 324 but also on impartial appointments, effective administration, consistent enforcement, transparency and public confidence.

Electoral integrity is a shared constitutional responsibility. The ECI administers elections, Parliament establishes the legal framework, courts review legality, political parties and candidates must comply with the law, and citizens sustain democracy through informed participation.
