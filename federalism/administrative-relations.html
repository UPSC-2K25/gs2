1. Constitutional Framework and Purpose

Administrative relations concern the distribution, coordination and exercise of executive power between the Union and the States. They translate the constitutional division of legislative powers into practical governmental action.

The principal provisions are contained in Part XI, Chapter II (Articles 256–263). They must be read with the distribution of executive power under Articles 73 and 162, the Union's duty under Article 355, the All India Services framework under Article 312, and the provisions concerning inter-State cooperation.

India's Constitution combines State-level executive autonomy with mechanisms for national coordination. Administrative relations therefore reflect both the federal division of responsibilities and the Constitution's preference for an effective Union capable of ensuring compliance with constitutional and parliamentary law.

2. Distribution of Executive Power: Articles 73 and 162

Article 73: Extent of Union executive power

  • Union executive power generally extends to matters on which Parliament has power to make laws.
  • It also extends to the exercise of rights, authority and jurisdiction conferred on the Union by treaties or agreements, subject to constitutional and legal limitations.
  • Where a matter falls within the State's legislative field, Union executive power does not ordinarily extend to it unless the Constitution or a parliamentary law expressly provides otherwise.

Article 162: Extent of State executive power

  • State executive power generally extends to matters on which the State Legislature has power to make laws.
  • In matters where both Parliament and State Legislatures can legislate, State executive power is subject to executive power expressly conferred on the Union by the Constitution or parliamentary law.

Practical significance

Legislative competence and executive authority are closely connected, but they are not identical in every situation. Constitutional provisions and valid legislation may confer administrative responsibilities on a government beyond the ordinary operation of the legislative lists.

3. Article 256: Obligation of States and Union Directions

Article 256 requires every State to exercise its executive power in a manner that ensures compliance with laws made by Parliament and with existing laws applicable in that State.

The Union executive may give directions to a State when necessary for this purpose.

Constitutional significance

  • It ensures that parliamentary legislation is implemented throughout the territory of India.
  • It creates a constitutional mechanism for addressing State inaction or non-compliance with applicable Union laws.
  • It strengthens national legal uniformity without generally transferring the State's entire administrative machinery to the Union.

Limits and concerns

Directions must have a constitutional or legal basis and cannot be treated as an unrestricted power to control State administration. Their use should respect the distribution of powers, federal conventions and the principles of constitutional governance.

4. Article 257: Union Control in Certain Cases

Article 257 provides that the executive power of every State must be exercised so as not to impede or prejudice the exercise of the executive power of the Union.

The Union may give directions to a State where necessary to secure compliance with this constitutional requirement.

Specific matters under Article 257

  1. Communications of national or military importance: The Union may direct a State concerning the construction and maintenance of means of communication declared by the Government of India to be of national or military importance.
  2. Protection of railways: The Union may direct a State concerning measures to be taken for the protection of railways within the State.

Financial consequences

Under Article 257(4), where a direction under clauses (2) or (3) results in additional costs for the State beyond those incurred in the normal discharge of its duties, the Government of India must pay an amount agreed between the governments. In the event of disagreement, the amount is determined by an arbitrator appointed by the Chief Justice of India.

Article 257A: Historical note

Article 257A, which had provided for Union deployment of armed forces or other Union forces in certain situations, was inserted by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976 and later omitted by the 44th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1978. It is not part of the present constitutional text.

5. Articles 258 and 258A: Delegation of Functions

Article 258: Union functions entrusted to States

  • The President may, with the consent of the State Government, entrust Union executive functions to that government or its officers.
  • Parliament may also confer powers and impose duties on a State or its officers in relation to matters within the Union's legislative competence.
  • The Union Government may agree to compensate the State for additional administrative costs arising from such entrustment.

Article 258A: State functions entrusted to the Union

  • A Governor may, with the consent of the Government of India, entrust State executive functions to the Union Government or its officers.
  • It provides a constitutional basis for administrative cooperation in the opposite direction.

Importance of delegated administration

  • Promotes administrative convenience and efficiency.
  • Reduces duplication of administrative structures.
  • Enables governments to use existing field-level machinery.
  • Supports coordinated implementation of public programmes.

These provisions demonstrate that federalism need not require completely separate administrative machinery for every governmental function.

6. Article 259: Omitted Provision

Article 259 was a transitional provision concerning the armed forces of certain States in Part B of the First Schedule. It was omitted by the Constitution (Seventh Amendment) Act, 1956 as part of the reorganisation of States and the constitutional changes associated with it.

It is relevant mainly for understanding the historical evolution of the Union-State administrative framework.

7. Article 260: Jurisdiction of the Union in Relation to Territories Outside India

The Government of India may, by agreement with the government of a territory not forming part of India, undertake executive, legislative or judicial functions vested in that government.

Such an agreement is subject to the law relating to the exercise of foreign jurisdiction and must be implemented in accordance with the constitutional provision.

Article 260 reflects the Constitution's capacity to accommodate arrangements involving the exercise of governmental functions beyond India's territory.

8. Article 261: Public Acts, Records and Judicial Proceedings

Article 261 promotes legal continuity and mutual recognition across the country.

  • Full faith and credit must be given throughout India to public acts, records and judicial proceedings of the Union and every State.
  • Parliament may determine the manner in which such acts, records and proceedings are proved and their effect determined.
  • Final civil judgments or orders delivered by civil courts anywhere in India are capable of execution throughout India according to law.

The provision supports national legal integration and reduces jurisdictional barriers in the recognition and execution of judicial decisions.

9. Article 262: Inter-State Water Disputes

Article 262 empowers Parliament to provide for adjudication of disputes relating to the use, distribution or control of waters of inter-State rivers or river valleys.

  • Parliament may provide for adjudication of such disputes by legislation.
  • Parliament may provide that neither the Supreme Court nor any other court shall exercise jurisdiction over such a dispute, subject to the constitutional and statutory scheme.

Parliament enacted the Inter-State River Water Disputes Act, 1956 to provide a mechanism for adjudication through tribunals. The River Boards Act, 1956 provides a separate statutory framework for the regulation and development of inter-State rivers and river valleys.

Water disputes demonstrate the close relationship between administrative coordination, federal trust, resource allocation and dispute resolution.

10. Article 263: Inter-State Council

Article 263 empowers the President to establish an Inter-State Council where it appears that public interests would be served by its establishment.

Functions that may be assigned

  1. Inquiring into and advising upon disputes that may have arisen between States.
  2. Investigating and discussing subjects in which some or all States, or the Union and one or more States, have a common interest.
  3. Making recommendations for better coordination of policy and action on such subjects.

Nature of the Council

  • It is a constitutional body when established under Article 263.
  • It is an advisory and recommendatory institution.
  • It is not a court or a binding adjudicatory tribunal.
  • Its effectiveness depends on consultation, political cooperation and follow-up by participating governments.

Establishment and composition

The Inter-State Council was established in 1990 by a Presidential Order following the recommendations of the Sarkaria Commission. Its composition is designed to facilitate dialogue between the Union and the States and among the States.

The Prime Minister chairs the Council. Chief Ministers of all States and the relevant Union Territory representatives, together with designated Union Ministers and other members specified in the order, participate in its deliberations.

Importance

  • Institutionalises cooperative federalism.
  • Provides a forum for discussing common policy concerns.
  • Can help resolve disagreements through consultation.
  • Encourages coordination before disputes become entrenched.

11. All India Services: Article 312

All India Services provide a common administrative cadre serving both the Union and the States. They are an important institutional link between national administration and State-level governance.

Constitutional mechanism

  • Article 312 empowers Parliament to create one or more All India Services common to the Union and the States.
  • The Rajya Sabha must pass a resolution supported by not less than two-thirds of members present and voting, declaring that it is necessary or expedient in the national interest to create such a service.
  • Parliament may then provide for the creation of the service by law.
  • The Indian Administrative Service and Indian Police Service are established All India Services.
  • Article 312 also recognises the possibility of creating an All India Judicial Service, subject to its constitutional conditions and limitations.

Constitutional and administrative significance

  • Promotes common administrative standards.
  • Facilitates coordination between Union and State governments.
  • Supports continuity and institutional experience.
  • Provides a pool of officers with experience across levels of government.

Concerns and criticisms

  • Tensions may arise between the officer's responsibilities to the State cadre and the broader national service structure.
  • Disagreements can emerge over deputation, transfers, disciplinary control and administrative accountability.
  • Excessive centralisation may weaken the perceived autonomy of State administrations.
  • Frequent transfers and political interference can affect neutrality, continuity and professional independence.

12. Article 355: Union Duty to Protect States

Article 355 imposes a duty on the Union to protect every State against external aggression and internal disturbance and to ensure that the government of every State is carried on in accordance with the provisions of the Constitution.

It is an important constitutional link between national responsibility and State administration.

  • It expresses the Union's responsibility to safeguard the constitutional order in the States.
  • It may be relevant to the Union's response to serious threats to public order or constitutional governance.
  • It must be read with other constitutional provisions, including Articles 256, 257 and 356.

Article 355 does not independently create an unrestricted power to displace elected State governments. Constitutional action must remain within the limits prescribed by the Constitution and judicially enforceable principles.

13. Article 365: Failure to Comply with Union Directions

Article 365 provides that where a State has failed to comply with, or give effect to, a direction given in the exercise of the Union's executive power under a constitutional provision, the President may hold that a situation has arisen in which the government of the State cannot be carried on in accordance with the Constitution.

Such a conclusion may be relevant to the operation of Article 356, but it does not make President's Rule automatic whenever a State fails to follow a Union direction.

The constitutional conditions for action under Article 356, the requirement of relevant material and the principles governing judicial review continue to apply.

14. Important Institutions for Administrative Coordination

Inter-State Council

A constitutional forum under Article 263 for consultation, discussion and recommendations on common interests and inter-State issues.

Zonal Councils

Zonal Councils are statutory bodies established under the States Reorganisation Act, 1956. They provide regional forums for cooperation on matters such as economic and social planning, border issues, linguistic minorities, transport and security.

They are chaired by the Union Home Minister, with Chief Ministers and other representatives participating according to the statutory framework.

North Eastern Council

The North Eastern Council was established under the North Eastern Council Act, 1971, as amended. It provides a regional platform for coordinated development and cooperation in the North-Eastern region.

National Development Council: Historical context

The National Development Council historically served as a forum for Centre-State discussion on planning and development. Its role should be understood in the context of the transition from the earlier planning framework to the institutional arrangements that followed the establishment of NITI Aayog.

15. Administrative Relations and Cooperative Federalism

Cooperative federalism requires the Union and States to work together in areas where responsibilities overlap or where national outcomes depend on State-level implementation.

Major mechanisms

  • Consultation through the Inter-State Council.
  • Regional coordination through Zonal Councils.
  • Delegation of functions under Articles 258 and 258A.
  • Common administrative services under Article 312.
  • Coordination in the implementation of parliamentary laws.
  • Joint responses to disasters, public health and security challenges.

Cooperative and coercive dimensions

Articles 256 and 257 enable the Union to issue directions in specified circumstances. Articles 258, 258A and 263, by contrast, provide important mechanisms for consensual cooperation and coordination.

A healthy federal arrangement requires both constitutional authority and institutional dialogue. Excessive reliance on unilateral directions can weaken trust, while inadequate coordination can undermine national implementation.

16. Judicial Principles and Important Cases

State of Rajasthan v. Union of India (1977)

The Supreme Court considered issues relating to the use of Article 356 and the federal relationship between the Union and the States. The judgment is part of the historical development of judicial review of central intervention.

S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994)

The Supreme Court strengthened constitutional limits on the use of Article 356. It recognised judicial review of a proclamation and emphasised the importance of testing a government's majority on the floor of the House in appropriate circumstances.

Its broader significance lies in protecting federalism and democratic accountability against arbitrary central intervention.

Government of NCT of Delhi v. Union of India (2018)

The Constitution Bench emphasised constitutional morality, representative government, collaborative federalism and the need for institutional respect between constitutional authorities. The judgment concerned the special constitutional arrangement governing the National Capital Territory of Delhi.

Government of NCT of Delhi v. Union of India (2023)

The Constitution Bench examined the distribution of control over services in Delhi under its special constitutional framework. The decision highlighted the relationship between democratic accountability, administrative control and the constitutional allocation of powers.

State of Punjab v. Principal Secretary to the Governor of Punjab (2023)

The Supreme Court addressed constitutional limits on the Governor's role in relation to the State Legislature. The decision is relevant to constitutional cooperation and the proper functioning of parliamentary institutions within the federal system.

General judicial principles

  • Constitutional powers must be exercised within their prescribed limits.
  • Federalism is a basic feature of the Constitution.
  • Constitutional authorities must act with institutional responsibility.
  • Discretionary or exceptional powers are not beyond judicial review.
  • Democratic accountability and constitutional procedures must be respected.

17. Major Issues and Challenges

1. Excessive centralisation

Broad or frequent use of Union directions can create a perception that State governments are administrative extensions of the Union rather than constitutionally empowered governments.

2. Political differences

Differences between the political leadership at the Union and State levels may affect consultation, implementation and institutional cooperation.

3. Institutional underutilisation

Irregular or inadequate use of consultative institutions can leave intergovernmental disagreements unresolved and weaken cooperative federalism.

4. Administrative accountability

Officers serving across levels of government may face competing expectations concerning political direction, professional neutrality and administrative responsibility.

5. Inter-State coordination problems

Disputes concerning water, boundaries, transport, migration, public health and environmental management require sustained coordination, reliable data and effective dispute-resolution mechanisms.

6. Overlapping responsibilities

Complex public programmes may involve multiple levels of government. Unclear responsibility can produce duplication, delays and disputes over accountability.

18. Reforms and the Way Forward

  1. Strengthen consultative federalism: Make regular and meaningful use of the Inter-State Council and Zonal Councils.
  2. Use Union directions with restraint: Ensure that directions under Articles 256 and 257 are reasoned, legally grounded and proportionate.
  3. Improve intergovernmental dispute resolution: Promote early consultation, mediation, reliable data-sharing and timely adjudication where required.
  4. Protect civil-service professionalism: Strengthen merit-based appointments, stable tenure, transparent deputation arrangements and accountability.
  5. Clarify administrative responsibilities: Use clear agreements, rules and implementation protocols where governments share functions.
  6. Improve regional cooperation: Strengthen regional forums to address cross-border development and infrastructure concerns.
  7. Promote constitutional trust: Encourage political dialogue and respect for the constitutional autonomy of each level of government.

20. Important Distinctions

Concept Distinction
Article 256 and Article 257 Article 256 concerns compliance with parliamentary and existing laws; Article 257 concerns preventing State executive action from impeding Union executive power.
Article 258 and Article 258A Article 258 concerns entrustment of Union functions to States; Article 258A concerns entrustment of State functions to the Union.
Inter-State Council and Zonal Councils The Inter-State Council is based on Article 263; Zonal Councils are statutory bodies under the States Reorganisation Act, 1956.
Article 262 and Article 263 Article 262 concerns adjudication of inter-State river-water disputes; Article 263 provides for consultation and coordination.
Article 355 and Article 356 Article 355 states a Union duty; Article 356 provides for a constitutional response when the government of a State cannot be carried on in accordance with the Constitution.
All India Services and Central Services All India Services serve both the Union and States through a common service framework; Central Services are Union services.

21. Integrated Revision Framework

Understand administrative relations through five connected dimensions:

  1. Distribution: Articles 73 and 162 define the general reach of executive power.
  2. Compliance and control: Articles 256 and 257 provide for Union directions in specified circumstances.
  3. Delegation: Articles 258 and 258A permit governments to entrust functions to each other.
  4. Coordination: Articles 261–263 and regional institutions facilitate legal continuity and intergovernmental cooperation.
  5. Administrative integration and constitutional duty: Articles 312, 355 and 365 connect national administration, State protection and constitutional compliance.

The central analytical issue is how India can maintain national coordination and constitutional compliance while preserving meaningful State autonomy and democratic accountability.
